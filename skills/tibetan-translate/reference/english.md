# The English standard (shared by prose and verse)

Accuracy at the word level is not the standard; **English that an English writer would write** is
the standard, with everything the Tibetan says in it. Every rule here came from a real failure.

## 1. The six gates

Run all six before printing. A version that fails one is revised, never printed with an apology.

1. **Read aloud.** Say it at speaking pace. Any backtracking, any sentence or line that needs a
   second pass, anything you could not say to a person and be understood the first time: failure.
   This gate outranks every tool.
2. **Idiom and collocation.** Every phrase: would an English writer, translating nothing, write
   this? Every verb: does English use it with that noun? Every image: an English idiom, or a calque?
3. **Clarity.** Line by line, sentence by sentence: say what it claims. If you cannot state it
   back, it does not communicate, however accurate it is.
4. **Logic.** Walk the relation tags. Every cause still a cause, every condition a condition, every
   source a source? Did any become a comma, an "and", or a full stop?
5. **Completeness.** Walk the construal and point at where each item lands: content words,
   negations, quantifiers, restrictives, honorifics, vocatives, tense, mood. Then back-construe:
   read the English cold and ask what Tibetan it implies.
6. **Register and form.** The declared register, all the way through; the declared mode's
   apparatus and no other; for verse, the measure (tibetan-verse Gate 6).

## 2. Collocation

| Tibetan | Dead rendering | English |
|---|---|---|
| `sgrib pa dag pa` | veils made clean | obscurations purified, dispelled, cleared |
| `tshogs bsags` | merit heaped | merit gathered, accumulated |
| `byin rlabs zhugs` | blessing inserted | the blessing enters, takes hold |
| `lta ba med` | holds no view | there is no view; no view reaches it |
| `sems la ltos` | look at the mind | look at your mind; watch the mind |

Test: would a competent writer ever put these two words together? If not, the phrase fails.

## 3. Idiom maps to idiom, never to gloss

A Tibetan image English does not have is rendered by the English expression with the same force,
and the source image is let go and noted. `thugs kyi nying khu` is not "the marrow of the heart";
English says *the very heart of*, *the lifeblood of*, *the quintessence of*. `zhabs pad brtan`
(lotus feet standing firm) is "may his life be long". `thugs rje'i chu rgyun` is "the unbroken flow
of compassion" or "ceaseless compassion". Keep a source image only where English carries it
unexplained: *the thicket of phenomena*, *the stake of grasping*, *the sentry of mindfulness* all
work; *marrow of the heart* does not.

## 4. Clarity

A sentence must make a point the reader can state back. *The heart's own meaning holds no view of
words* is grammatical and asserts nothing; *For the quintessential meaning there is no view
expressed by words* asserts something.

## 5. Relations are content

`las` source · `pas/bas` cause · `pa'i` modification · `gis/kyis` agent or means · `na` condition ·
`nas/te/ste` sequence or manner · `phyir` purpose or cause · `kyang` concession · `la` goal, locus,
or (after a verb) purpose. "Through this, that" may never become "this happens. That happens." A
long Tibetan period may become several English sentences; the connective is made explicit at the
seam, never dropped: `des na` → so, therefore · `phyir` → because, that is why · `kyang` → still,
even so · `pas` → since, so · `na` → if, when · `nas` → having …, then.

## 6. No inversion, no archaism, no filler

- **No inversion.** *The one from whom spring all the qualities* → *the one from whom all qualities
  arise.* No fronted objects ("him I praise"), no postposed adjectives ("the mind supreme"), no
  verb before subject. Detection: would you say it in that order out loud?
- **Banned:** thee, thou, ye, unto, behold (as a verb of seeing), lo, oft; word-shortening apostrophes
  (o'er, 'tis; ordinary possessives and ordinary contractions follow the house style); metrical or
  rhetorical filler ("truly", "indeed", "then", "all", "O", and "so" where it carries no `pas`/`des
  na`: "so" as the connective for a Tibetan cause is correct and wanted); stock dharma phrasing the
  Tibetan does not contain ("vast and profound", "boundless"); any image not in the Tibetan; noun
  piles with no finite verb; square brackets outside academic mode.
- **Preferred:** strong finite verbs over copulas and abstract nouns; the short concrete word
  ("rely on" not "have recourse to", "give up" not "relinquish", "ripen" not "come to fruition");
  Latinate and Sanskrit vocabulary only where it is the technical term.

## 7. Person, honorifics, names

- "You" wherever the Tibetan instructs or addresses; "one" only in genuinely general statements;
  never mixed in a sentence; never "he or she". Plurals where English wants them.
- Honorifics (`sku`, `thugs`, `gsung`, `mdzad`, `gsungs`, `bzhugs`) carry register and addressee
  status; they are realized by English register and verb choice, not by stacked adjectives. "The
  Buddha said", not "proclaimed the following words". Keep track of who is speaking to whom:
  `gsungs` vs `smras` tells you.
- Indian names in Sanskrit, Tibetan names phonetically; never invent a Sanskrit back-form. Titles
  of works by their fixed English short title (tibetan-citations).

## 8. Terminology

One stable rendering per term per project, from the glossary, with context-sensitive senses for
genuinely polysemous terms (the imperial `sgra sbyor` rule), each deviation noted. When a bound
term will not sit (in verse): re-measure, then unpack, then place it at line end, and only then
render differently and note the swap. Choose terms before drafting, not while repairing.

## 9. The failures that keep recurring

| # | Failure | Diagnostic | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dead collocation | would an English writer write this pair? | the verb English uses with that noun |
| 2 | Calqued idiom | does the image mean anything here? | the English idiom with the same force; note the image let go |
| 3 | Opaque sentence | can you state its claim? | make the assertion explicit |
| 4 | Inversion | verb before subject, object fronted? | normal order; let the form give |
| 5 | Flattened relation | has `las`/`pas`/`na`/`phyir` become a comma or "and"? | restore the connective |
| 6 | Reversed causation | `kho na las` as "alone"? | back-construe; source stays a source |
| 7 | Vanishing content | fewer lines/clauses than the Tibetan? | unpack, never compress |
| 8 | Scattered head | the head noun arrives after a complete phrase was parsed? | head first, modifiers after |
| 9 | Register flattening | instruction that does not instruct, aphorism that does not bite? | re-decide the register per unit |
| 10 | Overheating | elevated diction on plain material? | if the Tibetan is plain, the English is plain |
| 11 | Silent doubt | a construal that could go another way, unrecorded? | a `Q:` line |
| 12 | Coerced stress (verse) | the line reaches its count only against natural stress? | rewrite the line, not the reading |
| 13 | Verse filler read as content | `ni` as "indeed", `rnams` as an emphatic plural? | mark filler in the construal |
| 14 | Hallucinated Sanskrit | a back-form the text does not give? | translate the name instead |
| 15 | Wrong speaker | `gsungs` and `smras` collapsed, a vocative turned into a subject? | restore the speech act |
| 16 | Adverb calque | `mngon par` as "manifestly", `rab tu` as "thoroughly", `yongs su` as "completely" on every verb? | the plain verb; these prefixes mirror a Sanskrit preverb, not an English adverb |
| 17 | Nominal calque | "the later relies on the earlier", "the arising of X in the mindstream"? | a clause with a subject: "each relies on the one before", "X arises in your mind" |
| 18 | Demonstrative chain | "this text … this treatise … this essence" across a split period? | one noun, then pronouns; or restructure so the head noun comes once |
| 19 | Interposed adverbial | "he moistens, with the rain of the Dharma, the mindstreams"? | verb and object together; the adverbial before or after |
