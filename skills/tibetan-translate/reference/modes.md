# Audience modes and registers

Two independent settings. **Mode** is the audience's contract with the text (skopos: documentary
vs instrumental). **Register** is the genre's voice, decided per unit. Four registers on one page
is normal; one mode per edition is normal.

## 1. Modes (Pass 3 parameters)

| | Academic | New practitioner | Seasoned practitioner | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Contract | documentary, "thick": the reader sees the source through the translation | instrumental: the text works on its own for an educated non-specialist | instrumental, with a stable technical vocabulary the reader already owns | instrumental main text, thin documentary layer behind it |
| Sanskrit | IAST with full diacritics; technical terms may stay Sanskrit, italic at first use | translated wherever a good English term exists; the common dictionary words (karma, nirvana, sutra, mantra, buddha, bodhisattva) anglicized without diacritics | technical terms kept where the tradition uses them (dharmakaya, mahamudra, samaya, bhumi), anglicized, no diacritics, lowercase for common nouns | as seasoned, with IAST in the notes/glossary only |
| Apparatus | Wylie of key terms, square-bracket interpolations allowed, footnotes with sources and variants, witness sigla, secondary literature | none: no brackets, no Wylie, no locators in the body; a short glossary at the back if the project has one | none in the body; a back-matter sources register keyed by opening words; footnotes only for what the reader needs (an enumeration, a name, one opaque image) | as seasoned, plus endnotes for sources and construal choices |
| Epithets, honorifics | reproduced in full | simplified: one epithet, plain verbs ("the Buddha said") | carried where they are formulaic in the tradition; verbs plain | as seasoned |
| Repetition, stock passages | reproduced | may be compressed if the house style allows (say so) | reproduced | reproduced |
| Register ceiling | neutral-scholarly; no elevation | warm, direct, contemporary; the short concrete word | warm, direct, unhurried; elevated only where the Tibetan is (homage, supplication) | as seasoned |
| Verse | free verse line-for-line, numbered; or metred if the edition is for recitation | metred or free, line-for-line, per house style; readable aloud | metred (tibetan-verse), chant mode for liturgy | as seasoned |
| Person | the Tibetan's | "you" where the text instructs | "you" where the text instructs | as seasoned |
| Doubts | in the notes, with alternatives | silent in the body; reported to the editor | as new practitioner, plus a `Q:` for the editor | in the endnotes |

Models for each: academic = Hopkins/UMA, Wallace's Śāntideva; new practitioner = 84000 Reading
Room prose; seasoned = Padmakara, Library of Tibetan Classics; hybrid = Brunnhölzl.

Everything in a mode is a surface treatment of the same construal. A mode never adds, drops, or
reorders a relation, a negation, a quantifier, or a speech act.

**Academic mode is documentary in its apparatus, not in its syntax.** The six gates of `english.md`
apply in full: no calques ("the very own-nature", "wisdom-gnosis", "in that way, thus, … as well"),
no lemma-by-lemma order, no bracket-heavy clauses. Brackets mark a supplied word; they do not
license a word-for-word rendering. Measured 2026-10-03: a Hevajra-commentary page in academic mode
scored 3.3/5 on English against 4.2 for the same model on a seasoned-practitioner page, and the
gap was register, not accuracy. The commentary scaffold (`zhes pa ni … ste`) is kept as a scaffold,
and the gloss itself is written as an English sentence.

## 2. Registers (decided per unit)

| Register | Where it belongs | Person | Sentences | Diction | Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Instruction** (*man ngag*, practice manuals, "therefore do this") | the text turns to the practitioner | second, "you" | short, 12–18 words, imperatives | concrete, Anglo-Saxon, strong verbs | would a teacher say this to a student in the room? |
| **Exposition** (*rnam bshad*, *sa bcad* structure, definitions) | the text arguing or structuring | third or impersonal | longer periods, 20–30, connective-heavy | precise; technical terms exact | does the argument's shape survive one reading? |
| **Debate** (*mtshan nyid*, *thal 'gyur*, refutation) | the text contesting a view | impersonal | tight; every logical connective explicit ("it follows", "because", "if … then") | fixed technical vocabulary, no variation | can a reader reconstruct the syllogism? |
| **Commentary gloss** (*zhes pa ni … ste … go*) | the text explaining a root line | impersonal | the gloss scaffold kept: quote, "means", explanation | plain | is it clear which words are the root's and which the commentator's? |
| **Narrative** (*rnam thar*, the story behind a citation) | biography, framing history | third, past tense | plain, varied | ordinary, unadorned; honorifics by register not adjectives | does it move, or explain? |
| **Framing** (the sentence that introduces a quotation) | before every citation | minimal | one clause | formulaic: `In the *Title* it is said:` · `Name said:` · `as the *Title* says, "…"` · `And it is said:` | is it invisible? |
| **Liturgical** (prayer, supplication, dedication, sādhana) | verse to be recited | second, vocative; first for aspiration | measured, chantable | elevated but not archaic | can a group say it aloud together? |
| **Song / aphorism** (*mgur*, dohā, a line cited as proof) | lyric, pith, proverb | any | terse, often verbless in Tibetan | compressed, memorable, colloquial idiom allowed, refrains kept | does it land in one hearing? |
| **Colophon** | end of a text | third | formulaic | dating and lineage formulas kept | would a cataloguer find what they need? |

Mark a shift where it happens: `Issue: register shifts to instruction at "de bas na".`

## 3. The two register failures

- **Flattening.** Everything rendered as neutral exposition: the instruction stops instructing, the
  aphorism stops biting. Caused by translating the words and not the speech act.
- **Overheating.** Elevated diction applied to plain material because the subject is religious. A
  definition is a definition. If the Tibetan is plain, the English is plain.
