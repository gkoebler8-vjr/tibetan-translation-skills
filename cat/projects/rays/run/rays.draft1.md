# rays — Pass 1 draft (own reading, before grounding)

U01: For his own sake, he is the dharmakaya, which never shifts or changes: the element that is pure by nature, in which every adventitious stain has been cleared away. Not compounded by causes and conditions and free from elaboration, he abides like space in the equality of samsara and nirvana,

U02: and he possesses qualities such as the ten powers and the four fearlessnesses.

U03: For the sake of others, he is the sambhogakaya endowed with the five certainties, appearing as chief guide to his retinue of bodhisattvas, the Victor's regents.

U04: From the clouds of his great and loving compassion, his supreme nirmanakaya pervades the realm of beings, favoring none.

U05: It displays the twelve deeds, and with the rain of the sacred Dharma of peace and bliss it moistens the minds of disciples whose potential has fully awakened, and sets them free. I bow with reverence in body, speech and mind to the chief of the sugatas, Shakyasimha.

U06: Accordingly, in the Uttaratantra it is said:
  The Knower of the World, in great compassion,
  looks upon the whole of the world,
  and, never stirring from the dharmakaya,
  through emanated forms of many kinds
U07:
  he takes birth after birth,            [placeholder for skye ba mngon par skye ba]
  comes down from the Tushita heaven,
  enters the womb and is born,
  delights in his circle of queens,
U08:
  renounces the world, practices austerities,
  goes to the seat of awakening,
  crushes the hosts of Mara, fully awakens,
  turns the wheel of Dharma,
U09:
  and passes away into nirvana.
  He displays these deeds in impure realms
  for as long as existence lasts.

U10: Second. The scriptures of the supreme Sage, who conquered the four maras—the aggregates, the lord of death, the son of the gods and the afflictions—are free from the extremes of permanence and annihilation and as immeasurable as the ocean's depths. This treatise has been condensed from within them: from the sutras and the tantras, from the treatises of Nagarjuna, Asanga and others, and from the meaning of the profound pith instructions of the great Dharma Lord Drikungpa, which deserve to be honored like the jewel in a naga king's crown. It is free of the stains of not understanding, misunderstanding and doubt. Just as everything one needs or wishes for comes forth when one prays to the vaidurya, the finest of gems, so this treatise fulfills every aim, temporary and ultimate. It is the Heart of the Teaching, set out as ground, path and fruition, and I shall explain it.

U11: Now, of the four points—purpose and the rest—the subject matter is the three: ground, path and fruition.

U12: The purpose is to understand it through study and reflection. The ultimate purpose is that the fruition becomes manifest through meditation on its meaning. The connection is that each relies on the one before, and so, step by step, they are linked as means and what arises from the means.
