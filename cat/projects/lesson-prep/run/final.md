### U01

TEXT:
Today's topics

1. Contemplating death and impermanence

There are two kinds of impermanence, gross and subtle. The second, subtle impermanence, is the way things change from moment to moment.

How to meditate on your own death and impermanence

Three ways to meditate:
Meditating that I will certainly die.

none
NOTES:
Issue: source context assumed (no work or author named): a teacher's preparation notes for one class, in modern Tibetan with colloquial forms ('di ring, 'gyur 'gro, gsungs kyis yod); headings set as headings, definitions as sentences; only the first topic is numbered (༡), as in the source.
Issue: dang dang (dittography) read once; the notes define only the second kind (subtle); gross impermanence is named, never defined, and nothing is supplied.

Q: bdag in item 1 kept as "I" (the meditator's own thought; Gyaltsab's Four Hundred commentary phrases the meditation the same way: bdag ni nam 'chi nges pa med cing myur du 'chi'o snyam du sems pa, ACIP S5428I_T:451); alt. "you" / "one's own death".
Comm: no gloss of rags pa'i / phra ba'i mi rtag pa found (dm_gloss_U01.txt): Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen's Four Hundred commentary (ACIP S5428I_T:457–458) quotes the pair (meditate continually on both, after Potowa); NEAR only: Toh 4061, 51b-9 (momentary vs continuum impermanence), Toh 4016, 139b-35 (gross impermanence of a life seen directly, subtle inferred), consistent with the body. The three ways = the lamrim's three roots (rtsa ba gsum: Changkya, ACIP S0130M_T:761; TSD RDI-TOK-07:2403).

### U02
HEADER: U02 · prose (lesson outline: list items 2–3, two headings, an example) with a one-line verse citation: citation mode, rising, 3–4 beats; 1 pada, 1 line, order 1 · exposition; framing; aphorism · new practitioner · Toh 4089, Treasury of Abhidharma, D 10b (opening line of chapter 4), quoted as a paraphrase · grounding: the Treasury's own commentary (Toh 4090) gloss followed (DM primary search) · prior: none · confidence: high
TEXT:
Meditating that the time of death is uncertain.
Meditating that at death nothing but the Dharma will help.

On karma and its results

As the *Treasury of Abhidharma* says:
The world's variety comes from karma.

For example, even the outer world, the environment, comes from karma as its cause.

Contemplating the suffering of samsara
FOOTNOTES:
none
NOTES:
Source: Treasury of Abhidharma (chos mngon pa'i mdzod kyi tshig le'ur byas pa, Vasubandhu), Toh 4089, 10b-43, opening line of ch. 4: las las 'jig rten sna tshogs skyes; the notes quote it from memory as 'jig rten sna tshogs las las grub (order inverted, grub for skyes), translated as given (dm_identify_U02.txt, dm_identify_U02-2.txt, dm_cite_U02.txt). Register row P01/U02; TITLE row fixed.
Comm: the Treasury's own commentary (Bhāṣya, Toh 4090, 166a-3–7; the same in Toh 4091, 157a-11–15, and Toh 4095, 143a-21–24) reads 'jig rten sna tshogs as the many varieties of the world of beings and of the container world (sems can dang snod kyi 'jig rten), made by no one with prior intent and arising from the karma of beings; Gendun Drup (ACIP SL5525N_T:6430): las las snod bcud kyi 'jig rten sna tshogs pa skyes so. Followed: "the world's variety"; it also settles the example.
Q: rgyu las las grub: resolved by the Bhāṣya and KHEZ022:9760 (snod bcud kyi 'jig rten … rgyu gang las byung … las las): karma is the cause even of the container world, "comes from karma as its cause", taken; the alternative (a dittography for rgyu las grub, "comes from causes", cf. dang dang in U01) has no support.
Alt: phyi snod 'jig rten yang "the outer world, the environment, also / too" (MITRA "also"); "even" taken for the point the commentaries make (not only beings but their world).
Alt: 'khor ba sdug bsngal bsam pa "Contemplating that samsara is suffering".
Issue: snod (container) image let go: "the environment" (snod bcud = environment and inhabitants). Frame mngon pa mdzod las … gsungs pa ltar → "As the Treasury of Abhidharma says:" (block form of the inline formula; keeps ltar). The 7-syllable line set as a one-line verse block; beats.py with "variety" hand-counted (va-RI-e-ty, not in its lexicon); "The world in all its variety …" dropped (pulse fail, filler "all").
Comm: items 2–3: chos ma gtogs gang gis kyang mi phan in the lamrim works (TSD RDI-TOK-07:2403; Dwags po chos bzhi'i 'grel pa, TSD BEN-LB-017:704: chos kho na bya dgos) → chos = the Dharma one practises; "the Dharma" kept.
MITRA: agrees on content (rgyu las las "from the cause of karma"); turns items 2–3 and the closing heading into imperatives; the list form kept (sgom pa / bsam pa are nominal in the Tibetan).
Prior: none found.
Conf: high — the one fork (rgyu las las) resolved by the Treasury's own commentary; source verified; MITRA agrees on content.

### U03
HEADER: U03 · prose (one definition, a heading, a three-item list) · exposition · new practitioner · not a citation · grounding: Toh 3996 gloss followed; a lamrim commentary (TSD KN-LYN-02) makes the same move (DM primary search) · prior: none · confidence: medium
TEXT:
Once you recognize that samsara is by nature the three kinds of suffering, the wish to be free from it arises. This is what the teachings call renunciation.

The three kinds of suffering:
The suffering of suffering.
The suffering of change.
The pervasive suffering of conditioning.
FOOTNOTES:
FN(The three kinds of suffering): Pain is the suffering of suffering, passing pleasure is the suffering of change, and the suffering of conditioning is called pervasive because it runs through every feeling, even the neutral ones.
NOTES:
Q: agent of gsungs kyis yod (= colloquial gsung gi yod, honorific): the Buddha, the masters, or the class's own teacher; "This is what the teachings call renunciation" commits to none; alt. "This is called renunciation" / "the masters call this renunciation". Open.
Q: elided agent of shes par byed: generic "you" (lesson register); alt. impersonal ("Recognizing that samsara …").
Issue: source spellings read as nges 'byung (nges 'byungs) and gsung gi yod (gsungs kyis yod).
Comm: Toh 3996, 165a-18–26: pain = sdug bsngal gyi sdug bsngal, pleasure = 'gyur ba'i, neither = 'du byed kyi; khyab pa because it pervades every feeling (tshor ba thams cad la khyab pas); Toh 4421, 194a-19–194b-8, the same by feeling type; zhal gdams lam rim ye shes snying po'i rnam 'grel rab gsal nyin byed (TSD KN-LYN-02:1476–1490) makes U03's move (samsara never goes beyond the nature of the three sufferings → the wish to be quickly free of them). Followed: terms and reading; the footnote is written from Toh 3996, with "passing" from KN-LYN-02 (mi rtag g.yo ba).
Alt: khyab pa 'du byed kyi sdug bsngal "the all-pervasive suffering of conditioned existence".
MITRA: agrees (the same three terms; gsungs as "is taught as").
Prior: none found.
Conf: medium — one open Q on who "calls" it renunciation (honorific gsungs, no agent named); the English stays neutral.
