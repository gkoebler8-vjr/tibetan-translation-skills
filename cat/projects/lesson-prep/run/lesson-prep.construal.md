# lesson-prep — construal sketch (working material, never shipped)

SOURCE CONTEXT (assumed, inferred from the text): a teacher's (or student's) preparation notes for one
class, in modern literary Tibetan with colloquial forms ('di ring, 'gyur 'gro, gsungs kyis yod) and
typing slips (dang dang U01, nges 'byungs U03). Genre: lesson outline: headings, short definitions, one
inline citation. Content: the preliminary contemplations in lamrim order: death and impermanence (with
the three ways of meditating: certainty of death, uncertain time, only the Dharma helps), karma and its
results, the suffering of samsara, renunciation and the three kinds of suffering. Speaker: the teacher
of the class; addressees: the students. No named author or work; no prior translation expected.

## U01
'di ring gi brjod bya/ 1 'chi ba mi rtag pa bsam pa/ mi rtag pa la rags pa'i mi rtag pa dang dang phra ba'i mi rtag pa gnyis/ gnyis pa phra ba'i mi rtag pa ni/ dngos po rnams skad cig skad cig gi 'gyur 'gro ba/ rang nyid 'chi ba mi rtag sgom tshul/ sgom pa'i thabs gsum/ bdag nges par 'chi ba sgom pa/
FORM: prose outline, 8 shad-pieces: 4 headings (brjod bya; 1 … bsam pa; … sgom tshul; sgom pa'i thabs gsum), 2 definitional statements, 1 list item (first of three; items 2–3 open U02). Register: exposition (outline), plain.
SPINE: no finite verb. Verbless predications: [mi rtag pa la] … gnyis ("impermanence has two kinds"); [phra ba'i mi rtag pa ni] [dngos po rnams … 'gyur 'gro ba] ("subtle impermanence = things' changing moment by moment"). Verbal nouns: bsam pa (contemplating), sgom tshul (how to meditate), sgom pa (meditating that …).
RELATIONS: gi (brjod bya) = genitive "today's"; la (mi rtag pa la) = locus of division ("within impermanence: two"); rags pa'i / phra ba'i = modification; dang = coordination of the two kinds; gnyis pa … ni = topic, "the second, X, is …"; skad cig skad cig gi = distributive adverbial bound to the verbal noun ("from moment to moment"); rang nyid 'chi ba = self as subject of dying ("one's own death"); sgom pa'i thabs = genitive "ways of meditating"; bdag nges par 'chi ba = content of sgom pa ("that I will certainly die").
NEG/QUANT: gnyis (two kinds); gnyis pa (the second); gsum (three ways); rnams = plural "things"; nges par = certainly (modal, keep). No negation.
TERMS: 'chi ba mi rtag pa = death and impermanence; mi rtag pa = impermanence; rags pa'i / phra ba'i mi rtag pa = gross / subtle impermanence; dngos po = things; 'gyur 'gro = change; bsam pa (heading) = contemplating; sgom pa = meditate; brjod bya = topics.
Q: dang dang = dittography; read once (not a fork; Issue).
Q: rang nyid 'chi ba mi rtag sgom tshul: rang nyid = whose death ("your own death and impermanence"), taken: no ergative/genitive, rang nyid is the subject of the verbal noun 'chi ba, and item 1 (bdag nges par 'chi ba) confirms the focus on one's own death; alt. rang nyid = the meditator ("how you yourself meditate on death and impermanence").
Q: person: bdag (I) in item 1 = the meditator's own thought, kept as "I"; alt. generic "you"/"one's own" (MITRA: "the certainty of one's own death"). Heading rang nyid = "your own" (instruction); the switch your → I is the Tibetan's (rang nyid → bdag). Gyaltsab's first-person phrasing supports "I".
Q: 'chi ba mi rtag pa = coordinate set phrase "death and impermanence", taken; alt. "the impermanence that is death".
Issue: gnyis pa (the second) defined, the first (gross) never defined in the notes; kept as given, nothing supplied.
GROUNDING: DM primary search (dm_gloss_U01.txt): no gloss of the formula rags pa'i / phra ba'i mi rtag pa. QUOTE: Gyaltsab Darma Rinchen, Four Hundred commentary (ACIP S5428I_T:457–458): after Potowa, meditate continually on both gross and subtle impermanence, in the context of 'chi ba mi rtag pa; the same work phrases the meditation in the first person (S5428I_T:451: bdag ni nam 'chi nges pa med cing myur du 'chi'o snyam du sems pa) → supports keeping "I" in item 1. NEAR only (not cited as gloss): Toh 4061, 51b-9 (skad cig mar 'jig pa'i mi rtag pa vs rgyun gyi 'jig pa'i mi rtag pa); Toh 4016, 139b-35 (gross impermanence of one life seen directly, subtle inferred): consistent with the notes' definition of subtle impermanence. Body unchanged: none found.
GROUNDING (three ways, U01–U02; dm_gloss_U02-2.txt, NEAR hits, Tibetan lamrim works): the tradition's rtsa ba gsum: Changkya (ACIP S0130M_T:761) nges par 'chi ba bsam pa / nam 'chi cha med bsam pa / 'chi ba'i tshe chos ma gtogs gang gis kyang mi phan pa bsam pa ste rtsa ba gsum; lhag pa shes rab kyi bslab pa rim par phye ba (TSD RDI-TOK-07:2403, same in RDI-SS-05:3050): the three root contemplations, each with three branches. The notes' three headings are this formula; body unchanged.

## U02
nam 'chi cha med sgom pa/ shi tshe chos ma gtogs gang gis mi phan par sgom pa/ las 'bras skor/ mngon pa mdzod las/ 'jig rten sna tshogs las las grub/ gsungs pa ltar/ dper na phyi snod 'jig rten yang rgyu las las grub/ 'khor ba sdug bsngal bsam pa/
FORM: prose outline: list items 2–3 (cont. from U01), heading (las 'bras skor), inline verse citation (one 7-syllable pada) in a frame (mngon pa mdzod las … gsungs pa ltar), an example sentence, heading ('khor ba sdug bsngal bsam pa: opens the topic U03 develops). Register: outline exposition + framing; the citation is aphorism.
SPINE: [nam 'chi] [cha med] (when one dies — no certainty) → content of sgom pa. [shi tshe] [chos ma gtogs] [gang gis] [mi phan] → content of sgom pa ("at death nothing but the Dharma helps"). Quote: ['jig rten sna tshogs] [las las] grub (the varied world is produced from karma). Frame: mngon pa mdzod las … gsungs pa ltar (as the Kośa says). Example: dper na [phyi snod 'jig rten yang] [rgyu las las] grub.
RELATIONS: shi tshe = temporal "at death / when one has died"; ma gtogs = exception "except, nothing but"; gang gis … mi = instrument/agent + negation = "nothing (helps)"; par sgom pa = content complement; skor = topic "on, about"; las (after mdzod) = source of the quote; las las = karma + ablative = source "from karma"; gsungs pa ltar = "as (it) says" (manner, links the quote to the example); dper na = "for example"; yang = "even / also"; rgyu las las = "from karma as its cause".
NEG/QUANT: mi phan (negated, scope: gang gis = nothing at all helps); ma gtogs (exception); cha med (negated existential: no certainty); yang (even). No numbers.
TERMS: nam 'chi cha med = the time of death is uncertain; chos = the Dharma; las = karma; las 'bras = karma and its results; 'jig rten sna tshogs = the world's variety (draft had "the world in all its variety": PULSE fail and filler "all"); las las grub = comes from karma; phyi snod 'jig rten = the outer world, the environment (snod = container: image let go); rgyu = cause; 'khor ba = samsara; sdug bsngal = suffering.
Q: rgyu las las grub: (a) rgyu in apposition to las (karma) + ablative = "comes from karma as its cause", taken (it illustrates the quote, and yang = "even the outer world" needs karma as the point); (b) dittography for rgyu las grub "comes from causes" (cf. dang dang, U01). Ground in the Kośa's own commentary on the line (karma and the receptacle world).
Q: the quotation is not the canonical wording: the Kośa's line (as remembered) is las las 'jig rten sna tshogs skyes; translated as the notes give it (grub); identify.
Q: gsungs (in gsungs pa ltar): agent = the treatise/its author; "as the Treasury … says".
Q: 'khor ba sdug bsngal bsam pa: "the suffering of samsara", taken (heading form, parallel to 'chi ba mi rtag pa bsam pa); alt. "that samsara is suffering" (U03's definition reads samsara as by nature suffering).
Q: shi tshe = at death (shi = past stem: "when one has died / at the time of death"); "at death" covers both.
GROUNDING: Kośa root verified: Toh 4089, 10b-43 (las las 'jig rten sna tshogs skyes// de ni sems pa dang des byas//), opening line of ch. 4 (Bhāṣya 166a-1 closes ch. 3 just before); the notes paraphrase it ('jig rten sna tshogs las las grub). Gloss (dm_gloss_U02.txt): Bhāṣya Toh 4090, 166a-3–7 (same in Toh 4091, 157a-11–15; Toh 4095, 143a-21–24): yang sems can dang snod kyi 'jig rten rnam pa mang po sna tshogs pa … de sus byas she na/ sus kyang blo sngon du btang ste byas pa ni med do// … sems can rnams kyi/ las las 'jig rten sna tshogs skyes = the variety of the world of beings AND of the container world was made by no one with prior intent; it arises from the karma of beings. Gendun Drup (ACIP SL5525N_T:6430): las las snod bcud kyi 'jig rten sna tshogs pa skyes so; KHEZ022:9760: snod bcud kyi 'jig rten … rgyu gang las byung … las las. → resolves the Q on rgyu las las grub: branch (a), karma is the cause even of the container world; followed (body as drafted). Death items: chos ma gtogs gang gis kyang mi phan (RDI-TOK-07:2403; Dwags po chos bzhi'i 'grel pa, TSD BEN-LB-017:704: chos kho na bya dgos) → chos = the Dharma one practises; body unchanged.
MITRA (dm_translate_U01-U03.txt): agrees on content (reads rgyu las las as "from the cause of karma", yang as "also"); renders items 2–3 and the samsara heading as imperatives (speech-act drift; list form kept).

## U03
'khor ba de sdug bsngal gsum gyi rang bzhin du shes par byed nas de las thar 'dod kyi blo skye ba la nges 'byungs gsungs kyis yod/ sdug bsngal gsum/ sdug bsngal gyi sdug bsngal/ 'gyur ba'i sdug bsngal/ khyab pa 'du byed kyi sdug bsngal/
FORM: prose: one definitional period (3 clauses, ~37 syllables), then a heading and a three-item list. Register: exposition (definition), plain.
SPINE: (elided agent: the practitioner) shes par byed ['khor ba de … rang bzhin du] → nas → [de las thar 'dod kyi blo] skye ba → la → (elided honorific agent: the Buddha/masters/the teacher) gsungs kyis yod [nges 'byung] = "X arising is what (they) call renunciation".
RELATIONS: 'khor ba de = samsara (de resumes U02's closing heading); gsum gyi rang bzhin du = "as having the nature of the three sufferings" (adverbial of the knowing); nas = sequence: having recognized → then the wish arises (keep the order; not upgraded to cause); de las = source "from it (samsara)"; thar 'dod kyi blo = attitude of wanting to be free; skye ba la … gsungs = naming: "(the arising) is called"; gsungs kyis yod = gsung gi yod, colloquial present-habitual honorific "(they) say/call".
NEG/QUANT: gsum (three) twice. No negation.
TERMS: 'khor ba = samsara; sdug bsngal gsum = the three kinds of suffering; rang bzhin = nature ("by nature"); thar 'dod kyi blo = the wish to be free; nges 'byung = renunciation; sdug bsngal gyi sdug bsngal = the suffering of suffering; 'gyur ba'i sdug bsngal = the suffering of change; khyab pa 'du byed kyi sdug bsngal = the pervasive suffering of conditioning.
Q: agent of shes par byed: the practitioner, generic → "you" (lesson register); alt. impersonal ("recognizing samsara as …").
Q: agent of gsungs: the Buddha / the masters / the class's teacher (lesson notes); rendered "what the teachings call" (honorific realized by register, no person invented); alt. passive "is called".
Q: nges 'byungs = nges 'byung (final s a slip); gsungs kyis = gsung gi (colloquial auxiliary).
Q: khyab pa = "pervasive" (not kun khyab "all-pervasive"); bound term "the pervasive suffering of conditioning"; Alt: "all-pervasive suffering of conditioned existence".
GROUNDING (dm_gloss_U03.txt): Toh 3996 (rten cing 'brel par 'byung ba dang po dang rnam par 'byed pa bstan pa'i … bshad pa), 165a-18–26: sdug bsngal ni sdug bsngal ba'i sdug bsngal nyid … bde ba ni 'gyur ba'i sdug bsngal nyid … sdug bsngal yang ma yin bde ba yang ma yin pa ni 'du byed kyi sdug bsngal nyid … de khyab pa yin pa'i phyir ro zhes bya ba ni 'du byed kyi sdug bsngal la bya ste/ de ni tshor ba thams cad la khyab pas so = pain / pleasure / neither; "pervasive" because it pervades every feeling. Toh 4421, 194a-19–194b-8: same by feeling type (pleasant things: grief when parted from them; the rest: conditioned by causes and conditions). zhal gdams lam rim ye shes snying po'i rnam 'grel rab gsal nyin byed (TSD KN-LYN-02:1476–1490): the three; samsaric happiness mi rtag g.yo ba; then 'khor ba'i gnas gang du skyes kyang sdug bsngal gsum gyi rang bzhin las ma 'das pas de dag las myur du thar bar 'dod pa'i blo … bskyed par bya = the same move as U03's definition (samsara by nature the three sufferings → the wish to be free). Terms and reading confirmed; footnote on the three written from 3996/4421/KN-LYN-02. MITRA: agrees (same three terms; gsungs as "is taught as").
