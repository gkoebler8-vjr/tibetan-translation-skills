### U01
As for “At that time the Blessed One addressed the venerable Śāradvatīputra”:
Question: When so many—all of them—had come there, why did he address one alone? This also contradicts what was stated above: “[Each one] thinks, ‘The Tathāgata is seated in front of me and is teaching the Dharma.’” Suppose all who were gathered there also think, “The Tathāgata looked at me alone and taught the Dharma.” If the Blessed One singled out one among them all and addressed him, how, then, would that not contradict what the assembly thought?

### U02
Response: It is true that the Tathāgata taught with reference to all of them, making all of them directly present [to himself]. Nevertheless, in the instant he addressed Śāriputra, he was also addressing everyone. And when, in that instant of teaching, he also gave everyone different explanations of many kinds, the compilers were unable to compile that sequence. For this reason they set aside the splendor of the gnosis of [his] teaching, adopted a single sequence suited to all people, and compiled [the teaching] accordingly.

### U03
Alternatively, by teaching with reference to Śāriputra specifically, the Tathāgata also sanctioned [that way of teaching], since this very approach is a cause for the true Dharma to endure for a long time. Therefore the compilers, too, relied on just this and compiled [the teaching].

### U04
Question: Even granting that this is so, why, in teaching the supreme explanation of the bodhisattva vehicle, did he set the bodhisattvas aside altogether, address Śāriputra specifically, and open the discourse [with him]?

### U05
This should be understood as follows. The explanation of the *prajñāpāramitā* is a general explanation; it is not an explanation for the sake of the bodhisattvas in particular. If one asks why: because the prajñāpāramitā abides in three gateways—all-knowledge, knowledge of the aspects of the path, and knowledge of all aspects. In this context, had the Blessed One addressed any bodhisattva at all and opened the discourse [with him], the rest of the assembly might well have thought, “This profound [teaching] is not our domain,” and made no effort. But if he opens the discourse with reference to Śāriputra, the others will think, “This explanation is common to all people; it is not extremely profound.” Therefore he opened the discourse with reference to Śāriputra specifically. Accordingly, it is also said further on: “The monks and the rest of the assembly, too, clearly realized the Dharma and individually attained [the fruits].”

### U06
Qualm: On that point, even granting that this is so, the elder Śāriputra has done what had to be done. So it is not appropriate to open the discourse on this explanation of great import [with him].

### U07
But as for this claim that the elder Śāriputra has done what had to be done: in the *Saddharmapuṇḍarīka* the Blessed One predicted that the elder Śāriputra would become a buddha, saying, “Śāriputra, in the future, over incalculable, inconceivable, immeasurable eons, you will uphold the true Dharma of many hundreds of thousands of millions of billions of tathāgatas. You will also make offerings of various kinds [to them] and bring this very bodhisattva conduct to completion. Then, in the world [that is] the buddha field named Virajā, you will become the tathāgata, arhat, perfectly complete buddha called Padmaprabha, and you will appear in the world.”

### U08
Qualm: How could a monk whose contaminants are exhausted, and whose stream of karma and afflictions is cut off, take rebirth in a renewed existence and come to practice the bodhisattva conduct for inconceivable, unfathomable eons? This itself cannot even be understood. If arhats too were to take rebirth in a renewed existence, then all arhats would each take up a renewed existence, and so liberation would turn out to be unachievable.

### U09
But this does not necessarily follow. It is reasonable to say, “If arhats had karma and afflictions, they would also be born into existence through the power of karma and afflictions, and so liberation would not be achieved.” We, however, do not say that arhats in essence have karma and afflictions and are born into an existence produced by them.

### U10
The meaning will be explained at length following the sequence found in another sūtra. Here, the Tathāgata saw correctly that some beings are of definite lineage and others are of indefinite lineage.
