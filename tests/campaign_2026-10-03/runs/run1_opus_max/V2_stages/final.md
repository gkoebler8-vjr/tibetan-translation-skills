### U02
HEADER: U02 · prose · debate (objection and reply; honorific rhetorical question, lags sam) · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
You may wonder, "If that is so, what purpose does this serve, when it has become a ground for disputes between all sorts of conflicting positions?" As was said above, the Blessed One taught eighty-four thousand doors of Dharma. Is it not so that you enter by one of these doors and then go all the way to the innermost room?
NOTES:
Q: 'dis ci zhig bya taken as "what purpose does this serve"; alternative "what are we to do about this"; the reply fits both.
Q: de ltar gyur na placed inside the objector's thought; it could be the master's frame ("That being so, if you wonder …") with no change of sense.
Q: mi mthun pa sna tshogs rtsod pa'i gzhi taken as "disputes between all sorts of conflicting positions"; alternative "a ground on which all kinds of dissenters dispute".
Issue: the house image is kept through U06 (door → house of liberation → innermost room); phug = "the innermost room".
Check: in-context; 2 minor: zhugs nas made explicit as "and then go" (revised); snyam na carried by "You may wonder" and the reply that follows, the usual English for the debate formula (kept).

### U07
HEADER: U07 · prose · debate/rebuke with quoted sectarian speech · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
If that is so, those who have entered the door of the Tathagata's precious teaching and taken refuge in the Three Jewels take up particular practices that are not the same but differ from one another. All of them are equally Dharma. Yet they say, "Only this of mine is Dharma; that of his is not Dharma at all," "The Nyingma Secret Mantra is not Dharma," "Vajrapani is not Dharma," and "Amanasi, mental non-engagement, is not Dharma." By saying these and other such things, they have disparaged the word of the Tathagata.
NOTES:
Q: phyag na rdo rje: the deity's cycle, or (likelier, beside amanasi) the Indian master Vajrapani (b. 1017), Maitripa's disciple, who taught Mahamudra in Tibet; the bare name is kept and a footnote is recommended.
Q: a ma na se is the Tibetan transliteration of Sanskrit amanasi(kara), Maitripa's teaching on mental non-engagement; normalized to "amanasi".
Q: kho'i de kho na chos min: kho na taken as emphatic ("not Dharma at all"); the restrictive reading ("only his is not Dharma") is excluded by sense.
Q: the subject of skur ba btab is elided; taken as the same practitioners ("they"), that is, those among them who speak so.
Issue: gsum rendered "the Three Jewels" (the head noun dkon mchog is elided in the Tibetan and supplied).
Check: in-context; no findings.

### U08

### U09
HEADER: U09 · prose · exposition (the non-sectarian counterpart; generic "you") · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
So, if with the eye of the wisdom of hearing, reflection and meditation you see and hear a great many of the Sugata's scriptures, you will disparage none of them. Your eyes will be opened, and once you have become learned and gained understanding, you will become a great spiritual friend to all, taking them into your care.
NOTES:
Q: mthong ba dang / thos na: seeing and hearing both taken under the condition; alternative: "you see …; and when you hear, you disparage none".
Q: yongs su 'dzin pa taken as the spiritual friend "taking others into care" (the stock collocation with dge ba'i bshes gnyen); alternative: "embracing all the teachings fully", which also fits the non-sectarian theme.
Alt: mig rnyed pa ("gain the eye") rendered by the idiom "your eyes will be opened"; literal alternative "you will gain sight".
Check: in-context; 1 minor (mig rnyed pa, active in Tibetan, passive idiom in English), kept with the Alt line.

### U10
HEADER: U10 · prose: frame + prose citation (three sentences, set as a block) · framing; sutra prose · seasoned practitioner · Toh 343, Avadanashataka (Purnapramukha-avadanashataka), D 258a; quoted loosely
TEXT:
In the same vein, the Blessed One said in the *Hundred Avadanas*:

Those who abide in shamatha will be liberated through vipashyana. Those who abide in vipashyana will be liberated through shamatha. One who abides in both equally will be liberated through the elements.
NOTES:
Source: gang po la sogs pa'i rtogs pa brjod pa brgya pa, Toh 343, D 258a (segments 258a-13–15). The 46-syllable verbatim match from dm.py identify is the citing text itself (BO_EGS_0006); the canonical run is only 8 syllables, so the quotation is loose. The source reads "by cultivating calm abiding a great deal … one is liberated in dependence on insight", and its third sentence speaks of the mind of a noble listener who has fully cultivated both being liberated "by way of the elements".
Q: khams taken as plural "the elements" (the source names three: abandonment, dispassion and cessation, 258a-17); singular "the element" would leave room for a dhatu reading; a footnote naming the three is recommended.
Q: cha mnyam par gnas pa rendered "one who abides in both equally" ("both" supplied from the first two sentences); alternative "one who abides in balance".
Issue: English short title *Hundred Avadanas* coined for this edition (TITLE row in the glossary; register row in stages.sources.tsv).
Issue: the closing dang is realized as "And" at the head of U11.
Check: in-context; no findings. After the check, "So too" was changed to "In the same vein" to avoid "So … So too" after U09 (surface only; same relation).

### U11

NOTES:

Q: bogs 'don taken as "enhances" (the Kagyu term for enhancement); alternative "brings out the full yield of".
Issue: the closing pas is realized as "Therefore" at the head of U12.
Check: in-context; no findings.

### U12
HEADER: U12 · prose · conclusion, shifting to instruction · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
Therefore, for people whose minds have different ways in, putting different approaches to Dharma into practice becomes the antidote to afflictions and conceptual thoughts. Then comes liberation, and in the great ocean of liberation all phenomena become one taste. This is the crux of all the scriptures of the Buddha, the Blessed One. It is the subject matter of the three pitakas, the stages of practice. It delights the learned and makes the heads of the deluded spin; it is the domain of those whose realization is profound and vast. Understand this, and this alone, through and through.
NOTES:
Q: rnam par grol ba dang rnam par grol ba'i rgya mtsho chen por read as two coordinated stages, "liberation, and … one taste in the great ocean of liberation", mirroring U04–U06; alternative: one locative ("in liberation and the great ocean of liberation"), or a dittography.
Q: 'di kho na khong du chud par bya'o: the list is taken as predicated of the topic, with "this alone" as the object of "understand"; alternative: "understand that it is precisely this: the crux …".
Q: zab pa dang rgya che ba'i rtogs pa rnams taken as persons ("those whose realization is profound and vast", like mkhas pa rnams and rmongs pa rnams); alternative "profound and vast realizations".
Q: gang zag blo'i 'jug sgo tha dad pa la taken as "for people whose minds have different ways in"; alternative: causal ("since people's minds differ in their ways in").
Alt: mdo 'gag = "the crux"; or "the point where all … converge" (the word also means a confluence of roads).
Issue: the register shifts to instruction at khong du chud par bya'o ("Understand this …").
Check: in-context; no findings.
