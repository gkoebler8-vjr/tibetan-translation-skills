### U01
HEADER: U01 · prose (homage period, member 1 of 5; U01–U05 are one Tibetan sentence, head noun and main verb in U05) · homage, elevated · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
For his own benefit, the dharmakaya, never shifting or changing, abides like space in the equality of samsara and nirvana. It is the element, completely pure by nature, in which the adventitious stains have been entirely pacified; it is not compounded by causes and conditions, and it is free from elaboration.
NOTES:
Q: "in which" renders la after "completely pure by nature" as a locus; it may be additive ("pure by nature and, besides, with its stains pacified"). Both readings state the two purities.
Q: khams is read in apposition ("It is the element"); alternative: "whose element is completely pure by nature".
Q: "like space" goes with "abides" (the grammatical reading); alternative: "free from elaboration, like space".
Alt: "entirely pacified" — or "have ceased"; "abides in the equality" absorbs ngang ("the state of").
Issue: head first: the main verb ("abides") comes before the attributes, which keep their order; "his" anticipates Shakyasimha, who is named only in U05.
Check: in-context (no Agent tool): no finding in this unit; glossary aligned to "entirely pacified".

### U10
HEADER: U10 · prose (one period: outline marker + 6 sentences) · exposition, the commentator's first-person commitment · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
Second:
The supreme Sage has conquered the four maras — the aggregates, the Lord of Death, the son of the gods, and the afflictions. His scriptures are free from the extremes of eternalism and nihilism and as unfathomable as the depths of the ocean. The sutras and tantras within them, the treatises of Nagarjuna, Asanga, and others, and the meaning of the profound pith instructions of the great Dharma Lord Drikungpa deserve veneration like a naga king's crown jewel. From within these, this text has been condensed with no stain of incomprehension, misunderstanding, or doubt. Just as everything one needs and wishes for comes forth when one prays to the beryl, supreme among jewels, this treatise accomplishes all aims, temporary and ultimate. I shall explain this essence, set out as the three: ground, path, and fruition.

Q: "this essence, set out as the three" echoes the root text's title (theg chen bstan pa'i snying po); make the title explicit?
Q: "the great Dharma Lord Drikungpa" is Jigten Sumgön; footnote the identification?
Alt: "beryl" — or keep "vaidurya"; "the son of the gods" — or "devaputra".
Issue: the one period is split into six sentences; two attributive relatives (the Sage who conquered, the sources worthy of veneration) become predications, linked by "His", "within them", "From within these", and "this text / this treatise / this essence".
Check: in-context; no finding.
