U01: In this context, regarding the practice of meditation, the mantrin, in the morning, should visualize at their own heart a moon mandala, and upon it, the seed syllable of Nairātmyā together with a garland of light rays. Having performed the washing of the face and so forth, and having protected oneself, the place, and the yoga, one should enter the place of meditation, sit upon a comfortable seat, and by means of one’s own mantra, fully consecrate the argha. The mantrin should visualize the mandala of Nairātmyā, which gathers all the Tathāgatas, in the space before them, and should make offerings with the eight offering goddesses who have emerged from the seed syllable at their own heart.
U02: Gaurī holds the sign of the deer; in the hand of Caurī is the vessel of the sun; in the hand of Vetālī is water; in the hand of Ghasmarī is medicine.
U03: Pukkasī holds a vajra in her hand; Śavarī holds a corpse in her hand; Caṇḍālī beats a hand-drum; the tame one embraces [her] from the neck.
U04: In the presence of that [object of veneration], one should perform the confession of sins and so forth, saying: "I shall individually confess all my sins. I rejoice in all the virtues of the noble ones and of ordinary beings. Until I reach the essence of enlightenment, I go for refuge to the Buddha; I go for refuge to the Dharma; I go for refuge to the Saṅgha. For the sake of benefiting and bringing happiness to all sentient beings, and in order that they may attain the three types of enlightenment according to their capacity, I shall generate the mind directed toward unsurpassed, perfectly complete enlightenment."  Then, one should cultivate loving-kindness, wishing for all sentient beings to attain supreme happiness. Then, through compassion, one should remove all the suffering of those beings. Then, one should ensure that those beings are never separated from joy and happiness. Then, one should practice equanimity, which is the dedication of worldly and supramundane virtues to those beings.
U05: Then, all phenomena arise from the appearance of the mind; although these are not true, because the mind itself is deluded in terms of the grasper and the grasped, appearances arise like a dream. In order to definitely be free from this delusion, one should eliminate the appearances of objects such as blue, yellow, and so forth, and meditate upon the mind—which is devoid of the nature of appearing in such diverse ways—as being like the sky at midday in autumn, free of dust, infinite, clear, and without appearance. This is the ultimate bodhicitta, the supreme prajñāpāramitā, and this itself is the supreme wisdom of emptiness. The mantra for blessing this meditation is to recite: *oṃ śūnyatā-jñāna-vajra-svabhāva-ātmako 'ham*. Following that,
U06: From the *raṃ*, one should visualize a sun in front. Upon that sun, a *hūṃ* arises, which is a crossed *vajra*. By means of that very *vajra*, one should perform the visualization. One should bind the fence and the net.
U07: Then, one should meditate upon the *mudrā* of the great *dharmadhātu* (chos kyi 'byung gnas), which is white like the autumn moon, triangular, standing upright, and hollow within. In its center is the yellow, square *maṇḍala* of the Great Powerful One (*dbang chen*). Above that is the white, circular *maṇḍala* of water. Above that is the red, triangular *maṇḍala* of fire. Above that is the black, bow-shaped *maṇḍala* of wind. From the complete transformation of these, there arises a supreme celestial mansion (*gzhal yas khang*), blazing and produced from the five types of precious substances, resting upon a vast, variegated lotus and the hub of a variegated *vajra*.
U08: It has four corners and four doors, is adorned with four archways, and is surrounded by four ledges, where twice eight goddesses make offerings.
U09: Adorned with nets and half-nets, decorated with vajra-cords, the banners moved by the wind sound forth the ringing of bells at their various tips.
U10: Within its inner level, one should meditate upon an eight-petaled lotus. Upon its center and the petals of the cardinal directions, one should contemplate five seats of corpses. Regarding the outer level, there are the four intermediate directions and the four gates, and one below and one above.
U11: Upon the seat of that corpse, at the heart center of the central one, the mind of the meditator—previously cultivated, infinite, and clear like crystal—should be gathered into the form of a garland of the fifteen vowels; from the complete transformation of that, one should meditate upon a full moon. Above that, from a garland of the thirty-four consonants, it transforms into a sun mandala. Above that, from A, comes a curved knife (grigug). In the center of that is A. From that A, emanate all the Tathāgatas in the form of the fifteen Yoginīs, and having gathered them back into that very place, from the transformation of the moon, sun, curved knife, and seed syllable, one should meditate upon the Bhagavatī Nairātmyā.
U12: Of a dark color and very fierce, adorned with the five mudrās: the wheel, earrings, jewel, bracelets, and the belt.
