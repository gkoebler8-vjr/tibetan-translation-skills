# shastra — Pass 2 faithful drafts (from the construal)

### U01
Here, early in the morning, the *mantrin* engaged in meditation visualizes the seed syllable of Nairātmyā, together with a garland of light rays, on a moon disk at his own heart. Then he washes his face and so on, and protects himself, the place and the yoga. After that he enters the place of meditation and sits on a comfortable seat; then he consecrates the *argha* with his own mantra. The mantrin visualizes the maṇḍala of Nairātmyā, which gathers all the *tathāgata*s, in the sky in front. He should then worship it with the eight offering goddesses who arise from the seed syllable at his own heart.

### U02
Gaurī holds the deer-marked [moon];
in Caurī's hand, the vessel of the sun;
in Vetālī's hand, water;
in Ghasmarī's hand, medicine;

### U03
Pukkasī holds a vajra in her hand;
in Śavarī's hand, taste;
Caṇḍālī beats the hand drum;
Ḍombī embraces [her] around the neck.

### U04
In its presence one should perform the confession of wrongdoing and so on, as follows: "I confess all [my] wrongdoing. I rejoice in all the virtue of noble ones and ordinary beings. Until I reach the seat of awakening, I go for refuge to the Buddha; I go for refuge to the Dharma; I go for refuge to the Saṅgha. To benefit all sentient beings and make them happy, to bring them to attain the three kinds of awakening, each according to their lot, I generate the mind set on unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening." Then one should cultivate love, which wishes all beings to attain supreme happiness. Then, with compassion, one should remove all their suffering. Then one should make them inseparable from joy and happiness. Then [comes] the equanimity that brings them mundane and supramundane virtue.

### U05
Then [one reflects]: "All phenomena arise from mind appearing. None of these is real, yet through delusion mind itself appears as grasped object and grasping subject; it is like a dream." Thus, in order to be definitively free of delusion, one clears away the appearances as objects such as blue and yellow. Then one meditates on the mind—whose nature is free of the nature of appearing as variety—as infinite, clear and without appearance, like a dust-free sky at noon in autumn. This is the ultimate mind of awakening and the principal [deity], the Perfection of Wisdom; and this very [mind] is the gnosis of supreme emptiness. To bless this meditation by mantra, one recites *oṃ śūnyatājñānavajrasvabhāvātmako 'haṃ*. After that,

### U06
From raṃ one should visualize a sun in front,
and on that sun, from hūṃ, a crossed vajra;
with that very vajra one should visualize
and bind the fence and the net.

### U07
Then one should visualize the *mudrā* of the great source of dharmas (*dharmodaya*): white like the autumn moon, an upright triangle, hollow inside. In its center is the maṇḍala of Mahendra, square and yellow. On top of that is the maṇḍala of water, white and round; on top of that, fire, red and three-cornered; on top of that, wind, black and shaped like a bow. From their transformation [arises] a vast variegated lotus, and a celestial palace resting on the hub of a crossed vajra, supremely blazing and made of the five kinds of jewels,

### U08
four-cornered, with four doors,
adorned with four porticoes,
encircled by four railings,
where twice eight goddesses worship.

### U09
It is embellished with nets and half-nets,
adorned with vajra lines;
its banners, stirred by the wind,
ring the bells on their various tips.

### U10
In its inner ring one should visualize an eight-petaled lotus. On its center and on the petals of the [cardinal] directions one should imagine five corpse seats. [There are seats also] at the four intermediate directions and the four doors of the outer ring, and one below and one above.

### U11
At the heart of its central corpse seat, the meditator's mind, earlier meditated on as infinite and clear like crystal, is condensed into the form of a garland of the fifteen vowels, and from its transformation one should visualize a full moon. On top of that, the garland of the thirty-four consonants turns into a sun disk. On top of that, from *a*, [arises] a curved knife; in its center is *a*. From that *a* one emanates all the tathāgatas in the form of the fifteen *yoginī*s and gathers them into that very *a*. From them, now become the moon, the sun, the curved knife and the seed syllable, one should visualize the Blessed Lady Nairātmyā.
