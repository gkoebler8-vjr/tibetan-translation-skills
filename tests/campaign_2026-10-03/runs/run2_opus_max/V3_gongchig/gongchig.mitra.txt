U01: Therefore, based on the certainty of the pith instructions for meditating on the meaning of the mind, if one engages in practice while supplicating the Lord of the World (Jigten Gönpo) and his spiritual heirs with intense devotion, then it does not appear that it would be very difficult to follow in the footsteps of the masters of old.
U02: In this way, Drikung Lingpa also stated: "Those who have sealed these viewpoints and gained full certainty, and who know how to apply the intention of all the Sage’s teachings to their own mental continuums in harmony and have understood them, are the heirs born from the heart of that unequaled father, the protector Drikungpa. They are inconceivable and marvelous, enjoying their share of the Dharma."
U03: These are the ways in which all things are included within the five phenomena. Fifth, if one asks why it is called the "single intention," regarding that, there is the general single intention and the specific single intention.
U04: As for the first: according to the *Khog dbub chen mo*, for example, the four great rivers of Jambudvīpa, together with their assemblies of five hundred subordinate rivers, as well as streams, ponds, springs, waterfalls, and other such diverse types of water—which have distinct colors, distinct smells, distinct functions, and differing potentials—when they have reached the great ocean, they are all the same in having a salty taste.
U05: Having regard for the differing elements, faculties, and interests of sentient beings, the eighty-four thousand heaps of Dharma, the three collections, the four classes of tantra, the twelve branches of the scriptures, and the various entryways of the vehicles and paths that were taught—there is nothing among all of these that is not included within the great ocean of the *dharmatā*, the meaning of the ultimate, single, unborn *mahāmudrā*; therefore, it is called the "single intention."
U06: By Zhwa dmar Chos kyi grags pa: Because it clearly demonstrates that the sacred Dharma of the assembly of the victorious ones of the three times and the nature of their teachings are harmonious and without contradiction, the name "Single Intention" (dgongs gcig) is also widely renowned.
U07: As it has been said: because all the victorious ones of the three times, even when teaching the Dharma through the various different languages of gods, humans, and others, or through words that are extensive, summarized, and so forth, do not differ in that they teach while holding the unborn fundamental nature as the core of their intention, it is called the "single intention" (*dgongs pa gcig*).
U08: The second point, the unique *gong pa gcig pa* (single intention), is as stated in the *Khog dbub chen mo*: "It is necessary to add a supplement to that former [point]; what that is, is the assertion of the Incomparable Lord: Mahāmudrā is the causality of emptiness." And furthermore: "It is not sufficient to meditate only on the unborn; in order to abandon the confusion of having wandered in saṃsāra for a long time, one must combine skillful means and profound wisdom. Therefore, it is of utmost importance to teach the limitless gateways of skillful means, such as the three vows."
U09: As it is said, if one thinks that all the buddhas have taught only emptiness and that this alone is what is to be meditated upon, it is taught that this is the work of Māra.
U10: Just as it is said in the *Anavatapta-paripṛcchā-sūtra*: "Furthermore, there are two. What are the two? They are wisdom devoid of skillful means and skillful means devoid of wisdom; these are the two. Among these, wisdom devoid of skillful means is this: not looking toward all sentient beings and conceptualizing about uncompounded phenomena."
U11: Skillful means devoid of wisdom are as follows: the four means of gathering that have fallen into the view of grasping at objects. Because it is said that those two are the activities of Māra, a bodhisattva should completely abandon them.
U12: Therefore, because it is established that the intention of all buddhas is one, not only in teaching the unborn fundamental nature but also in teaching the Dharma of means—the infallible causality—it is called the "Single Intention" (*dgongs pa gcig pa*). These are the etymological explanations of the mode of the Single Intention.
