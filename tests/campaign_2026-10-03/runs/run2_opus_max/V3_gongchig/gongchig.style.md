### U01
Therefore, if you are certain that this is the pith instruction for meditating on the nature of mind, and engage in it while supplicating Jigten Gönpo and his spiritual sons with intense devotion, following in the footsteps of the forebears does not look all that hard.

### U02
Accordingly, Drikung Lingpa said:
Those who bear the seal of these views and are convinced of them, who know how to apply them to their own mindstream in harmony with the intention of all the Sage's words, and who have come to understand them — they are sons born from the heart of the peerless father, the Protector Drikungpa. Inconceivable and marvelous, they enjoy their share of the Dharma.

### U03
That is how everything is included in the five teachings.
Fifth, if you wonder why it is called the Single Intention: there is the single intention in general, and there is the single intention in particular.

### U04
First, in the *Great Overview* it is said:
For example, in Jambudvipa there are different kinds of water: the four great rivers with their retinue of five hundred tributaries, streams, ponds, springs, waterfalls and so on. Each has its own color and smell and its own function, and their properties differ. When they reach the great ocean, all of them become one in the taste of salt.

### U05
In the same way, the Buddha taught with the different dispositions, faculties and inclinations of beings in mind. He taught the eighty-four thousand collections of Dharma, the three pitakas and the four classes of tantra, the twelve branches of scripture, and the different gateways to the vehicles and paths. Of all that he taught, there is not one teaching that does not flow into the great ocean of dharmata, the meaning of unborn mahamudra, in which they are ultimately one. That is why it is called the Single Intention.

### U06
And Shamar Chökyi Drakpa said:
Because it clearly shows the intention
In which the teaching's lines of descent agree
And do not conflict with the sacred Dharma
Of the hosts of victors of the three times,
It is widely known by the name Single Intention.

### U07
As these passages say, all the victors of the three times teach the Dharma through various means — in the different languages of gods, humans and others, in words extensive or concise, and so on. Yet whatever the means, they teach holding the unborn basic nature as the core of their intention. Since there is no difference among them in this, it is called the Single Intention.

### U08
Second, the single intention in particular. In the *Great Overview* it is said:
Something must be added to the former. What is it? It is the view of the peerless Lord: mahamudra — emptiness and cause and effect.
And it is said:
Meditating only on the unborn is not enough: to give up the delusion of long wandering in samsara, skillful means and profound wisdom must be united. That is why it is extremely important to teach the countless gateways of skillful means, such as the three vows.

### U09
As these passages say, if you think that all the buddhas taught only emptiness, and that likewise only emptiness is to be meditated on, this is taught to be the work of mara.

### U10
For, as it is said in the *Sutra Requested by Anavatapta*:
Furthermore, there are two. What are the two? Wisdom without means and means without wisdom: these are the two. Of these, wisdom without means is this: disregarding all sentient beings and contemplating unconditioned phenomena.

### U11
Means without wisdom is this: the four ways of gathering disciples, fallen into a view that fixes on reference points. These two are the work of mara; bodhisattvas must give them up.

### U12
Therefore, it has been established that all the buddhas are of one and the same intention not only in teaching the unborn basic nature but also in teaching the Dharma of means — unfailing cause and effect. That is why it is called the Single Intention. This is the explanation of the name Single Intention.
