## U01
As to "the divisions of saṃvara, and so on": saṃvara means consecration (abhiṣeka). The detailed classification of that [consecration] is the "division" (dbye ba); that is, it has the characteristic of being what will be explained. As is said of this:

## U02
For the benefit of beings
consecration is proclaimed as fourfold.
Since by this one is washed and consecrated,
it is therefore called "consecration."
So it was taught.

## U03
In order to state the detailed classification of those same four consecrations, it is also said as follows:

## U04
Master, secret, and wisdom,
and thus, likewise, the fourth too.
[The classification of the four consecrations] will be explained by [the verse] that begins with these words.

## U05
In this yoginī tantra, one is a master (ācārya) because one practises fully what is far from sinful and nonvirtuous things; for that very [conduct] is the saṃvara of that [master]. The essence of the consecration given by the master is what is called "the accomplishment of the intrinsic nature itself of the four joys by the four moments, from the karmamudrā." It is not [the case], however, that, because [that consecration] is shared with the Kriyā tantras and the rest, one who has received the irreversible consecration is [already] consecrated by the master's consecration and so is not to be consecrated [again]. Therefore the statement "One thus also comes to have the fortune to expound, hear, and so on, all the yoga and yoginī tantras" is of no account. Therefore, in the first place, in order to gain authority over hearing, meditating on, and so on, the yoginī tantras such as the Hevajra, one should be consecrated by the master's consecration itself. Moreover, it should be understood that it is indicated by the characteristics of the master, the secret, wisdom, and the fourth, as in the realization (abhisamaya).

## U06
To those of dull faculties who have received such a master's consecration, meditation on the karmamudrā and on the mudrā of elaboration is to be taught, once they have grasped it with certainty through conviction. Likewise, on the side of the completion stage as well: experiencing the four joys, whose nature is the four moments, through the guru's instruction itself, the jewel that stays hidden, is called the "secret consecration," because it is not to be taught to yogins engaged in the meditation on elaboration. Here, to those whose faculties for consecration are middling, meditation on the samayamudrā is to be taught and explained.

## U07
Likewise, the distinguished gnosis (jñāna) is wisdom (prajñā): the knowledge that all phenomena are only mind. The consecration for the sake of accomplishing it is the wisdom-gnosis (prajñājñāna) consecration. Moreover, it should be understood that wisdom-gnosis is the correct indication of the external mudrā and the moments, through the guru's instruction itself, in the form-bearing jewel of the heart, which gathers the essence of the three consciousnesses (dependent, imagined, and transformed). Through that consecration, to those whose faculties for consecration are superior, the dharmamudrā is to be taught and explained by means of an illusion-like samādhi.

## U08
Likewise there is also a fourth, as [the words] "thus likewise too" (de ltar de bzhin yang) [say]. "That" (de) means wisdom-gnosis. "Likewise" (de bzhin) means suchness (tathatā); "the true limit" and "dharmadhātu" do not differ in meaning. The consecration by which one sees its very essence, or accomplishes it, is what the word "likewise" is to express. The word "too" (yang) means that [it] should also be given following the wisdom-gnosis consecration. Moreover, as for the apprehending of the external mudrā and of the perfection of the moment (which has the characteristic of not being delimited object by object through the guru's instruction itself) by the yoga of apprehending without apprehending: the mere giving of the instruction on this, which is to be called the "fourth consecration," does not make it the fourth consecration. Otherwise, if the Blessed One, by [the lines] beginning "Washed by this and consecrated," intends consecration here to be the washing away of stains, how could it come about merely through the teaching of an instruction?

## U09
If it is said, "If reality (tattva), whose nature is the fourth, were taught, how would it not become [the fourth consecration] in that case?" that is true. Nevertheless, because reality is not within the domain of speech, it cannot be expressed, and it cannot be accomplished through hearing either. Of this it is said as follows:

## U10
The very reality that is clear within
is heard and seen directly from speech.
Yet in the sound there comes to arise
a reflection of conceptual thought.

## U11
By meditating on a reality imputed
by one's conceptual thoughts expanding, this and that,
if conceptual thoughts are stirred up in the mind-stream,
how could reality be discerned?
This is what was taught.

## U12
If it is said, "If through consecration reality is directly realized and all stains without exception are purified, then why would the accomplishment (siddhi) of mahāmudrā not be achieved in that very moment?" it is indeed achieved by those of sharp faculties whose diligence blazes.
