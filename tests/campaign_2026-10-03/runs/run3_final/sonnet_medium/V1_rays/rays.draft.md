### U01
HEADER: U01 · prose (opening of the homage period U01–U05) · homage, elevated as the Tibetan is · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
He who, for his own benefit, is the dharmakaya — unchanging, pure by the nature of the expanse, its adventitious stains pacified, not compounded by causes and conditions, free of elaboration — and who abides, like space, in the state where samsara and nirvana are the same,
NOTES:
Issue: the homage is one Tibetan period whose verb "I bow" comes in U05; the English carries it as "He who … who …", resumed by "to him" in U05, to avoid fronting or splitting the sentence.
Q: "like space" is taken with the abiding; alternative, with "free of elaboration" or with the sameness.
Q: khams rang bzhin gyis rendered "by the nature of the expanse"; alternative "pure in its own element, by nature".
Q: la after "pure" taken as a converb with the stains as an absolute phrase; alternative, locative ("in which the adventitious stains are pacified").

### U05
HEADER: U05 · prose (closes the homage period; main verb "I bow") · homage · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
who shows the twelve deeds; who moistens the mindstreams of disciples whose family has awakened with the rain of the sublime dharma of peace and happiness, and liberates them — to him, the Lion of the Shakyas, chief of the sugatas, I bow in devotion with body, speech, and mind.
NOTES:
Q: rigs read as "family" (gotra); alternative, "type" of disciple.
Q: shakya seng ge rendered as the epithet "Lion of the Shakyas"; the editor may prefer Shakyamuni.
Issue: yang dag par (before sad pa) let go as a plain "awakened"; zhi bde dam pa'i rendered "sublime … of peace and happiness".

### U06
HEADER: U06 · verse citation, 7-syllable padas; citation mode, rising, 3–5 beats; this unit 4 padas, 4 lines, order 1-2-3-4 · framing, then exposition in verse · seasoned practitioner · Toh 4024, Uttaratantra (Tengyur), quoted in the commentary itself; the Tibetan lacks the canonical pada "bzo yi gnas la mkhas pa dang"
TEXT:
In the *Uttaratantra* it is said:
With great compassion he knows the world,
and having looked on all the world,
without stirring from the dharmakaya,
through emanations of many natures:
NOTES:
Source: dm.py identify, no verbatim match for the whole as quoted (50-syllable run); Toh 4024 (and the commentary Toh 4025) hold it; one sentence runs U06–U09.
Issue: de yang absorbed into the citation formula.
Q: 'jig rten mkhyen taken as a finite verb with the Buddha as agent; alternative, the epithet "knower of the world".
Q: sprul pa'i rang bzhin rendered "emanations of … natures"; "he" supplied for the Buddha, the agent of ston in U09.

### U08

### U09

### U10
HEADER: U10 · prose, one long period split into five English sentences · exposition (commitment to explain) · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
The second part. The words of the supreme sage, who triumphed over the four maras — the aggregates, the lord of death, the son of the gods, and the afflictions — are free of the extremes of permanence and annihilation and are as immeasurable as the expanse of the ocean. From these words, and from among the sutras and tantras, the treatises of Nagarjuna, Asanga, and others, and the profound meaning of the pith instructions of the great Dharma Lord of Drikung — all as venerable as the jewel on a naga king's crown — this treatise has been gathered without the stains of nonunderstanding, wrong understanding, and doubt. Just as all that one needs and wishes for comes about when one prays to lapis lazuli, the foremost of jewels, so it accomplishes all aims, temporary and ultimate. I will explain it, the essence taught as ground, path, and fruit.
NOTES:
Q: head noun: "this treatise, the essence taught as ground, path, and fruit" (apposition, chosen) against "the essence of the treatise, taught in three".
Q: chos rje 'bri gung pa chen po is rendered by title; if the editor confirms Jigten Sumgön, name him.
Q: gnyis pa ni read as "the second part" (the commitment to explain after the homage); 'di read as the root text, rendered "this treatise".
Issue: copula "are" supplied in the first sentence; las ("from these words") and nang nas ("from among") are both kept.

### U12
HEADER: U12 · prose · exposition (structural scaffold) · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
The purpose is to understand this through study and reflection. The ultimate purpose is that, by meditating on its meaning, the fruit becomes manifest. As for the connection: relying on the earlier, the later is linked to it step by step, as a means is linked to what arises from that means.
NOTES:
Q: snga ma and phyi ma are left general ("the earlier", "the later"); the referent may be the purpose and ultimate purpose, or ground, path, and fruit.
Alt: dgos pa'i dgos pa, literally "the purpose of the purpose".
