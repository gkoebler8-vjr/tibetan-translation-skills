### U05
HEADER: U05 · prose · exposition (mandala symbolism, "X is Y" glosses) · seasoned practitioner · source: not a citation
TEXT:
Of these, the square is the four immeasurables. The four doors are the four truths. The four porticos are the four dhyanas. The eight pillars are the eight limbs of the noble path. The single cornice is one-pointedness of mind itself. The lotus is the womb. The many colors are the many emanations. The nine deities are the nine limbs of the scriptures. The red direction is great passion. In the intermediate directions, white, yellow, green and black are the brahmin, vaishya, kshatriya and shudra castes themselves. The moon and sun are semen and sweat. The sword at the center is the emblem of black Acala. The curved knife is the Vajra Lady of Hatred. The directions beginning with the east are the white Acala and the others. The intermediate directions beginning with the fire corner belong to the Vajra Lady of Delusion and the others. This is the purification of the mandala.
NOTES:
Q: 'phar ma gcig pa rendered "single cornice"; alternatives lintel, ledge.
Q: rngul is "sweat"; in context it may stand for the female fluid.
Alt: rta babs "porticos" (archways).
Issue: names of the two vajra ladies translated, no Sanskrit back-form supplied.

### U06
HEADER: U06 · prose · exposition (symbolic glosses) · seasoned practitioner · source: not a citation
TEXT:
I shall state the purification of meditation. First, offering is the accumulation of merit, the supreme action. Emptiness is the accumulation of primordial awareness. Death, the supreme stainless body, is the gandharva body. The palace, in its ultimate sense, is the buddha ground. The lotus is the womb. The moon and sun are the white and the red.
NOTES:
Q: the syntax of 'chi ba khyad par 'phags pa dri ma med pa'i lus te dri za'i lus so is uncertain; "death" as a body is odd and the text may be corrupt.
Q: gzhal yang khang read as gzhal yas khang (palace); mthar thug pa rendered "in its ultimate sense".
Issue: brjod par bya ste rendered as first person "I shall state".

### U07
HEADER: U07 · prose · exposition (symbolic glosses with a narrative of conception) · seasoned practitioner · source: not a citation
TEXT:
The syllable HUM is the mind of the gandharva between mother and father. Akshobhya is the father. Mamaki is the mother. Seeing their mutual attachment, he feels hatred for the father and, attached to the mother, enters out of delusion, like a sentient being. The son who has entered the lotus kills the father and attains his position. Having taken the mother, through the supreme bliss of the imprints of endless births, he too brings about his birth as a son. The daughters, again, are the white Acala and the others, and also the Vajra Lady of Delusion and the others. The sons, too, having killed the father, go to others and, saying "this very one is the enemy," kill him. The daughters, too, feel desire through the supreme bliss of the imprints of endless births.
NOTES:
Q: de yang bur skye bar byed do: subject and sense uncertain; read as "he too brings about his birth as a son". MITRA has simply "he is born as a son"; kept, since byed do marks a causative or active sense.
Q: the referent of "him" in the killing of the enemy (the father, or the one called enemy).
Q: "go to others" read as going to other women.
Q: bu mo (daughters) are identified with Acalas and ladies as the text gives.

### U08
HEADER: U08 · prose · exposition (iconographic glosses) · seasoned practitioner · source: not a citation
TEXT:
The sword is wisdom and the noose is means. Or else, the noose is wisdom and the sword is means. The two having become one taste is the threatening finger. The left, looking downward, means protecting the seven underworlds. The right, looking upward, means protecting the seven eggs of Brahma. The left knee set on the ground means protecting the earth. The right foot stamping down means terrifying all the maras. The mara of the aggregates is Brahma; the mara of the afflictions is Ishvara; the mara of the lord of death is Vishnu; the mara of the son of the gods is Indra, lord of the gods.
NOTES:
Q: the subject of "the left" and "the right" is elided (hand, eye or foot).
Q: tshangs pa'i sgo nga bdun rendered "seven eggs of Brahma".
Issue: names Brahma, Ishvara, Vishnu, Indra are the standard Sanskrit for the Tibetan titles.

### U12
HEADER: U12 · verse: citation mode, rising, 3–4 beats; padas 7 × 4, four lines, order 1-2-3-4 · aphorism/exposition · seasoned practitioner · source: not a citation
TEXT:
The Teacher, the one and only Victor,
abides in the manner of five.
The perfection of wisdom as well
abides in the fivefold manner.
NOTES:
Q: ston pa rgyal ba read as apposition ("the Teacher, the Victor"); alternative "the Teacher alone is the Victor". MITRA reads gcig pu nyid as a predicate ("the Victor is unique"); kept apposition, the Tibetan has no copula to support the predicate.
Alt: yang dag gnas rendered plain "abides" (alternative "truly abides").
