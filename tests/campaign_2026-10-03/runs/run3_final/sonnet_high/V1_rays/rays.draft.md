# rays.draft.md  (draft v1, after Pass 3 style: house style applied: no contractions, no brackets, anglicized Sanskrit, phonetic Tibetan)
Note for the checker: in the Tibetan, U01-U05 are ONE sentence whose main verb 'dud do (I bow) stands at the end of U05. In the English the main clause is placed first (in U01) and U01-U05 then continue with finite clauses whose subjects are the three kayas of the Buddha named in the homage (head noun first, no fronted object). U06-U09 are ONE sentence whose verb ston (shows) stands at the end of U09; in English the verb and the head noun "deeds" (from mdzad rnams) are brought forward into the last line of U06.

### U01
I bow with reverence in body, speech, and mind to the Lion of the Shakyas, chief of the sugatas. For his own benefit, his dharmakaya abides, like space, in the state of the equality of samsara and nirvana. It is unchanging, its basic element naturally pure, its adventitious stains pacified, not compounded by causes and conditions, and free of elaboration.

### U02
It is also endowed with qualities such as the ten powers and the four fearlessnesses.

### U03
For the benefit of others, his sambhogakaya, endowed with the five certainties, appears as the chief guide of the retinues of the regent bodhisattvas.

### U04
From the cloud of his great, loving compassion, his supreme nirmanakaya pervades the realm of beings without partiality.

### U05
Displaying the twelve deeds, the nirmanakaya moistens the mindstreams of the disciples whose disposition has been rightly awakened, with the rain of the sublime dharma of peace and happiness, and brings them to liberation.

### U06
In the *Uttaratantra* it is said:
Through great compassion he knows the world,
and, having looked on all the world,
without stirring from the dharmakaya,
through varied emanations he shows his deeds:

### U07
taking manifest birth,
leaving the Tushita heaven,
entering the womb and being born,
delighting with the retinue of queens,

### U08
renouncing the world, practicing austerities,
going to the seat of awakening,
defeating the host of maras,
becoming perfectly awakened,
turning the wheel of the dharma,

### U09
and passing into nirvana —
in the impure realms,
for as long as samsara remains.

### U10
Second: The Supreme Sage is the conqueror of the four maras — the aggregates, the lord of death, the son of the gods, and the afflictions. His teachings are free of the extremes of eternalism and nihilism and are immeasurable like the expanse of the ocean. They include the sutras and tantras, the treatises of Nagarjuna, Asanga, and others, and the meaning of the profound pith instructions of the Great Drikungpa, Lord of Dharma, all of them worthy of veneration like the crown jewel of the naga king. From among them this compendium has been condensed. It is free of the stains of non-understanding, wrong understanding, and doubt. Just as every need and wish arises when one makes supplication to the beryl, the supreme of jewels, so it accomplishes all aims, temporary and ultimate. I shall explain this treatise, the essence taught as the three: ground, path, and fruit.

### U11
In this regard, of the four topics beginning with purpose, the subject matter is the three: ground, path, and fruit.

### U12
The purpose is to realize it through hearing and reflection. The ultimate purpose is that, by meditating on its meaning, the fruit becomes manifest. The connection is this: each later one relies on the one before it, and they are linked in sequence in the way a means is linked to what arises from that means.
