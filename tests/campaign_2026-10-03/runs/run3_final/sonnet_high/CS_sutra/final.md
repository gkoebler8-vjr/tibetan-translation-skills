### U01
HEADER: U01 · prose (10 clauses, enumerated list) · sutra exposition, formulaic repetition kept · new practitioner · not a citation (Toh 147, ch. 2)
TEXT:
Moreover, son of noble family, the tathagata's aspiration does not decline. What is this aspiration? Namely, aspiration for virtuous qualities. And what are these? The tathagata's aspiration for great love does not decline. The tathagata's aspiration for great compassion does not decline. His aspiration for teaching the Dharma does not decline. His aspiration for taming beings does not decline. His aspiration for ripening beings does not decline. His aspiration for seclusion does not decline. His aspiration for inspiring beings to embrace awakening rightly does not decline. His aspiration for not cutting the lineage of the Three Jewels does not decline.
NOTES:
Q: rigs kyi bu rendered "son of noble family" (kulaputra); alternative "noble son".
Q: dge ba'i chos = "virtuous qualities"; alternative "virtuous practices".
Q: byang chub yang dag par 'dzin du 'jug pa: yang dag par taken with 'dzin (embrace rightly); alternative "to embrace perfect awakening".
Alt: rab tu dben pa "seclusion" (plain; alternative "complete seclusion"); yongs su smin pa "ripening" (yongs su not rendered as an adverb).
Issue: the eight items are set one per sentence, numbers not added; "his" for the elided tathagata after the first two items.

### U03

### U04
HEADER: U04 · verse: free, line for line, 4 padas of 15 syllables, 4 lines, order 1-2-3-4 · doctrinal verse · new practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
He does not act out of desire, hatred, fear, or delusion.
Having become distinguished, the wise one comes through that knowledge to realize all that has been done.
Seeing beings who lack aspiration and are lazy, the Victor engages them in aspiration.
He knows the workings of beings' aspiration with unequaled knowledge.
NOTES:
Q: pada 2 is obscure: khyad par gyur nas = "having become distinguished" (alternative "becoming excellent"); byas pa'i chos kun = "all that has been done" (alternative "all made phenomena"); the knowledge is "that" knowledge.
Q: pada 4: gang gis taken as the agent (the Victor) of rig; 'dun pa'i las = "the workings of aspiration" (alternative "karma of aspiration"; alternative las as "than").
Issue: gang gis ... rig (by whom ... is known) set as an active sentence.
Q: MITRA reads byas pa'i chos as "constructed phenomena" and 'dun pa'i las as "actions based on aspiration"; grammar allows both, kept "all that has been done" and "workings of aspiration".

### U05
HEADER: U05 · prose (about 9 clauses) · sutra exposition · new practitioner · not a citation (Toh 147, ch. 2)
TEXT:
Moreover, son of noble family, the tathagata's diligence does not decline. The tathagata's diligence is as follows: it is diligence in not abandoning the beings to be tamed, and diligence in having no contempt for those who listen to the Dharma. If the tathagata finds a vessel of the Dharma, a listener who does not grow weary of hearing the Dharma even for an eon, then the tathagata too teaches the Dharma. Even when he goes without food, he does not rise for an eon. Because the tathagata looks upon beings, he goes so far as to cross and travel through buddha fields as many as the grains of sand in the river Ganges, even for the sake of a single being whom a buddha can tame, wherever that being may be. The tathagata has no fatigue of body, speech, or mind.
NOTES:
Q: de bzhin gshegs pa yang chos ston: "too teaches the Dharma" (matches the listener who is a vessel); alternative "even teaches for an eon".
Q: chos kyi snod chos nyan pa = "a vessel of the Dharma, a listener" (apposition); alternative a vessel who listens.
Q: tha na gang na sangs rgyas kyis 'dul ba'i sems can gcig = "a single being whom a buddha can tame, wherever"; sangs rgyas generic ("a buddha"), alternative the tathagata himself.
Q: yal bar mi 'dor = "not abandoning" (alternative "not letting fall away").
Alt: skyo ba "weary", mnyel ba "fatigue" (kept distinct).

### U09
HEADER: U09 · prose (about 14 clauses, question-and-answer) · sutra exposition · new practitioner · not a citation (Toh 147, ch. 2)
TEXT:
Moreover, son of noble family, the tathagata's mindfulness does not decline in any way at all, in any respect whatever. Why is that? The tathagata has no forgetfulness.
Son of noble family, as soon as the tathagata awakens to unsurpassed, perfect awakening, he looks upon the mindstreams of the beings of the past, the future, and the present. Even so, he has no forgetfulness regarding them at all. Even though he knows the conduct of beings exactly as it is, the tathagata does not let go of wisdom either. It is through arranging beings into three groups, entering into the faculties of beings, entering into their dispositions, and looking upon their conduct that the tathagata's mindfulness does not decline. The tathagata does not teach the Dharma to beings after recalling, thinking, or deliberating. Even while he teaches, his mindfulness does not decline.
Why is that? The tathagata's mindfulness is undiminished in this way: just as he himself is mindful and without forgetfulness, so he teaches the Dharma to beings. This is the twenty-third of the tathagata's activities. Concerning this, the following is said:
NOTES:
Q: thams cad kyi thams cad ... rnam pa thams cad kyi thams cad du is a reduplicative idiom of totality; rendered "in any way at all, in any respect whatever".
Q: phung po gsum = "three groups" (rasi), not the five aggregates; the object (beings) is supplied.
Q: ye shes yang gtong ba med: "does not let go of wisdom either"; alternatives gtong as "exert, apply", yang as "even".
Q: de ji ltar nyams pa med do: rendered "undiminished in this way: just as ..."; alternative a question, "How is it undiminished? Just as ...".
Q: ma thag tu ... gzigs kyang: tense set as a doctrinal present; "Even so" carries kyang across the sentence break.
Issue: dran par byas sam bsams sam rnam par brtags te: "recalling, thinking, or deliberating", no object added; the subject of the elided clause "Even while he teaches" supplied as his mindfulness.
Q: MITRA reads ye shes gtong ba med as "does not have to exert his wisdom" and ji ltar nyams pa med do as a separate sentence ("in any way"); kept "let go of wisdom" and "in this way: just as ...", grammar does not decide the first.
