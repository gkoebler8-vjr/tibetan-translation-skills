U01: For one’s own benefit, [the Buddha] abides in the state of the *dharmakāya*, which is without transition or change, in the element that is naturally pure, where adventitious stains are completely pacified, which is uncompounded by causes and conditions, and which, being free from conceptual elaborations, is like the sky, in the equality of existence (*saṃsāra*) and peace (*nirvāṇa*).
U02: endowed with qualities such as the ten powers and the four fearlessnesses, and
U03: For the sake of others, the Saṃbhogakāya, endowed with the five certainties, appears as the chief leader guiding the retinue of bodhisattva regents, and...
U04: From the clouds of great loving compassion, the supreme emanation body (nirmāṇakāya) pervades the realm of sentient beings without partiality, and—
U05: Having revealed the twelve deeds, and having moistened the mindstreams of those disciples whose lineage has been perfectly awakened with the rain of the holy Dharma of peace and bliss, thereby bringing about their liberation, I bow with respect—with body, speech, and mind—to the Lion of the Śākyas, the principal among the Sugatas.
U06: Furthermore, as stated in the *Ratnagotravibhāga*:  With great compassion, he knows the world; Having gazed upon the entire world, Without wavering from the *dharmakāya*, Through various emanated natures,
U07: Manifesting birth, descending from the Tuṣita realm, entering the womb, being born, and delighting in the company of the royal consort’s retinue.
U08: Renouncing the world and practicing austerities, proceeding to the essence of enlightenment, vanquishing the hosts of Māra, and perfectly turning the wheel of the Dharma of enlightenment;
U09: Those who have passed into *nirvāṇa* manifest as remaining within the entirely impure realms for as long as cyclic existence (*saṃsāra*) endures; thus it was said.
U10: As for the second: I shall elucidate the heart of the treatise that teaches the ground, the path, and the result, which accomplishes all temporary and ultimate purposes, just as the supreme of all jewels, the vaiḍūrya, grants all needs and desires when supplicated. This work, which summarizes the profound meaning of the scriptures of the Supreme Sage—who has conquered the four māras, namely the aggregates, the Lord of Death, the son of the gods, and the afflictions, and whose teachings are free from the extremes of eternalism and nihilism and are as immeasurable as the depths of the ocean—as well as the sutras, tantras, the treatises of Nāgārjuna, Asaṅga, and others, and the profound pith instructions of the great Chöje Drikungpa, which are worthy of veneration like the crown jewel of the king of nāgas, has been compiled free from the stains of non-understanding, misunderstanding, and doubt.
U11: Furthermore, among the four topics such as the purpose and so forth, the subject matter consists of the three: the ground, the path, and the result.
U12: As for the purpose, it is to realize that through study and reflection. As for the purpose of the purpose, it is the manifestation of the result through meditating upon the meaning of that. As for the connection, it is connected in the manner of the former being relied upon, and the latter arising sequentially as the method and that which is produced from the method.
