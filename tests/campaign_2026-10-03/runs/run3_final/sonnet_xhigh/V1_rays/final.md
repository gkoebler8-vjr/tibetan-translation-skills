### U01
HEADER: U01 · prose (U01–U05 are one Tibetan period, split in English) · homage, expression of worship; elevated but current · seasoned practitioner, publication (reader edition) · not a citation
TEXT:
For his own benefit the dharmakaya abides in the state where samsara and nirvana are equal. It does not change; its basic element is pure by nature, while its adventitious stains are completely pacified; it is not produced by causes and conditions, and it is free of elaboration, like space.
NOTES:
Q: chos sku — "the dharmakaya abides" (bare noun as subject; changed from "he abides as the dharmakaya" after the MITRA flag) vs. Shakyasimha as the one subject of the whole relative chain, the kaya a predicate.
Q: khams rang bzhin gyis rnam par dag pa la — "its basic element is pure by nature, while its adventitious stains are completely pacified" vs. locus ("in the naturally pure element the stains are pacified", MITRA's reading) vs. plain "and".
Q: nam mkha' ltar — "free of elaboration, like space" (set last so it can read with the whole) vs. attached to "abides".
Issue: one Tibetan period (U01–U05) is split into English sentences; the owner of the three kayas, Shakyasimha, is named only in U05 ("his" here is cataphoric); the seams (shing, dang, cing) are carried by "And".
Issue: ngang du bzhugs brought to the front of the sentence (head first); the attributive chain on chos sku became predications.
Check: in-context; 1 minor (the coordination at zhing was lost at a sentence break; joined with a semicolon). MITRA differs (dharmakaya as "state", khams as locus); subject changed to the kaya, rest kept.

### U05
HEADER: U05 · prose (end of the period; main verb 'I bow') · homage · seasoned practitioner, publication (reader edition) · not a citation
TEXT:
And he displays the twelve deeds and, with the rain of the sublime Dharma of peace and happiness, moistens the mindstreams of the disciples whose potential has awakened, and brings them to liberation. In reverence I bow, with body, speech, and mind, to him, Shakyasimha, chief of the sugatas.
NOTES:
Q: rigs yang dag par sad pa — "whose potential has awakened" (rigs = gotra; yang dag par absorbed in the verb) vs. literal "lineage" or "family" (MITRA: "lineage ... perfectly awakened"); kept.
Q: subject shift — U01–U04 have the three kayas as subjects, U05's converbs (bstan te, brlan te, mdzad pa'i) take Shakyasimha, since mdzad pa'i modifies him; rendered "he", resolved by the name at the end.
Q: mdzad pa bcu gnyis su bstan — "displays the twelve deeds" vs. "shows himself as the twelve deeds".
Alt: shakya seng ge = "the Lion of the Shakyas" (Shakyasimha kept as the attested epithet; the Tibetan does not say Shakyamuni).
Issue: honorifics (mdzad, gshegs, 'dud) are carried by register; "I" is supplied for 'dud do (grammar, not invention).
Check: MITRA differs (rigs = lineage; reads the converbs with "I" as subject, "thereby" for te); kept.

### U06
HEADER: U06 · verse: citation mode, rising, 3–4 beats; padas 7 × 4, four lines (frame in prose), order 1-2-3-4 · quoted authoritative verse, first stanza of one sentence · seasoned practitioner, publication (reader edition) · Uttaratantra (rgyud bla ma), Toh 4024; quoted with one canonical pada omitted
TEXT:
And in the *Uttaratantra* it is said:
With great compassion he knows the world
and, having looked on the whole world,
without stirring from the dharmakaya,
through emanations of many natures—
NOTES:
Source: named by the commentator as the rgyud bla ma. dm.py identify: verbatim match NO for the lines as given (longest shared run 50 syllables) because one canonical pada is missing; Toh 4024 (Uttaratantra) matches in two runs of 50 syllables and the canon text read in context confirms the rest; also quoted in its commentary, Toh 4025. Locator for the sources register: Toh 4024. English short title fixed here: *Uttaratantra*.
Q: mkhyen stands bare — "he knows the world and, having looked ..." (taken) vs. "he who knows the world ..., having looked ..., shows" (relative).
Q: subject number — "he" (Shakyasimha, as in U05) vs. "they" (the buddhas generally, as in the Uttaratantra).
Q: sprul pa'i rang bzhin sna tshogs kyis — "through emanations of many natures" vs. "emanated forms of many kinds".
Issue: frame — de yang = "And"; ces gsungs so (end of U09) folded into the introducer "it is said:" (citation formula).

### U10
HEADER: U10 · prose (one long Tibetan period, split into seven sentences) · exposition, commitment to explain; the commentator speaks · seasoned practitioner, publication (reader edition) · not a citation
TEXT:
Second: the supreme sage is the victor over the four maras—the aggregates, the lord of death, the son of the gods, and the afflictions. His Word is free of the extremes of permanence and annihilation and is as immeasurable as the expanse of the ocean. This treatise has been condensed from that Word, free of the stains of non-understanding, wrong understanding, and doubt. It comes from within the sutras and tantras, the treatises of Nagarjuna, Asanga, and others, and the profound meaning of the pith instructions of Chöje Drikungpa Chenpo. All of these are worthy of veneration, like the jewel on the crown of the naga king. Just as everything one needs and wishes for comes about when one prays to the vaidurya, the best of jewels, so this treatise brings about all aims, temporary and ultimate. I will explain it, the essence taught as the three of ground, path, and result.
NOTES:
Q: head noun — bstan bcos ... snying po as apposition ("it, the essence taught as the three", taken) vs. genitive "the essence of the treatise" (MITRA: "the heart of the treatise") vs. snying po as the title word, "Essence".
Q: scope of "worthy of veneration, like the jewel on the crown of the naga king" — the whole list (taken) vs. only the Drikungpa's pith instructions (MITRA attaches it to the instructions).
Q: 'di = the root text (not named in the Tibetan); the agent of bsdus pa is not stated (passive: the root text's author, implied) vs. "I have condensed".
Q: chos rje 'bri gung pa chen po — kept as a Tibetan name (Chöje Drikungpa Chenpo), not identified; the editor may name him.
Q: gnyis pa ni — "Second:" kept as a bare label; the first heading (the homage) carries none in the text as given.
Q: las ... nang nas — two stages of selection (from the Word; from within the sutras and the rest) rendered as "from that Word" and "from within".
Alt: bai dur ya = "beryl" (vaidurya kept); gnas skabs = "immediate"; dgos 'dod = "all one needs and wishes"; rtag chad = "eternalism and nihilism"; klong = "depths" (MITRA).
Issue: the attributive chain on the sage and his Word became predications in splitting ("is the victor", "is free of"); the connectives are carried at each seam (las, nang nas, ltar, na with bzhin).
Check: MITRA differs at the head noun and the scope of the simile; kept.
