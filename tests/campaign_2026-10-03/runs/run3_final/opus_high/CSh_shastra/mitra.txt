U01: Regarding the divisions of the vows and so forth: the vow is the empowerment. Its detailed division is the division, which possesses the characteristics that will be explained. That is also as it is said:
U02: For the sake of benefiting sentient beings, the empowerments (*abhiṣeka*) are proclaimed to be four. Because by this one is washed and empowered, therefore it is expressed as being called "empowerment." Thus it was spoken.
U03: In order to state the detailed classification of those very four empowerments, it is as follows:
U04: The master, the secret, the wisdom, and likewise the fourth, will be explained by [the passage] beginning with [the words] "so-called."
U05: In this Yoginī Tantra, one is called a "master" (ācārya) because one has become far removed from sinful and non-virtuous phenomena through one's conduct; this is because it is the vow (samaya) of that very state. The essence of the master’s empowerment is the perfect accomplishment of the nature of the four joys through the four moments (kṣaṇa) by means of the action mudrā (karmamudrā). It is not the case that one should perform the master’s empowerment [for those who have] already obtained the irreversible empowerment, because that is common to the Action Tantra and so forth. Therefore, the statement that "one becomes a candidate for explaining, listening to, and so forth, all the Yoga and Yoginī Tantras" is not the case [if one has not received this specific empowerment]. For this reason, one must first be empowered by the master’s empowerment itself, in order to be authorized to listen to, meditate upon, and so forth, the Yoginī Tantras such as the *Hevajra*. Furthermore, it should be understood that this is characterized by the four [empowerments]—the master, the secret, the wisdom, and the fourth—just as they are in the realization (abhisamaya).
U06: For those of dull faculties who have obtained such an *ācārya-abhiṣeka* (master’s initiation), one should, having firmly grasped it through devotion, teach the cultivation of the *karmamudrā* and the *prapañcamudrā*. Likewise, regarding the aspect of the *niṣpannakrama* (completion stage), that which is the experience of the four joys, which are of the nature of the four *kṣaṇas* (moments), by means of the *upadeśa* (instruction) of the guru—the jewel abiding in secret—is termed the *guhyābhiṣeka* (secret initiation), because it is not to be taught to yogins who are engaged in the cultivation of *prapañca* (elaboration). Here, for those of middling faculties of initiation, the cultivation of the *samayamudrā* should be specifically taught and explained.
U07: In the same way, the superior primordial wisdom is wisdom (*prajñā*), which is the knowing of all phenomena as mere mind (*cittamātra*). The empowerment for the sake of fully accomplishing that is the wisdom-primordial wisdom empowerment (*prajñājñānābhiṣeka*). Furthermore, one should understand that this is the wisdom-primordial wisdom, which, by the very pith instruction of the guru, perfectly indicates the external mudrā and the moments within the heart-jewel, which has the form of the essence of the three consciousnesses—the other-dependent (*paratantra*), the imagined (*parikalpita*), and the perfected (*pariniṣpanna*)—collected together. By that empowerment, for those of the highest faculties who are being empowered, one should demonstrate and explain the dharma-mudrā through the illusion-like meditative absorption.
U08: Likewise, the fourth is also [as described]; it is called "thus" and "likewise." "That" refers to the wisdom of pristine cognition (*prajñājñāna*). "Thus" refers to suchness (*tathatā*), and there is no difference in meaning between that, the ultimate limit (*bhūtakoṭi*), and the expanse of reality (*dharmadhātu*). That by which one sees or fully accomplishes its essence is what is expressed by the term "thus." The term "likewise" means that it should be bestowed immediately following the empowerment of the wisdom of pristine cognition. Furthermore, that which is the fourth empowerment is not merely the instruction of the term; it is the yoga of non-conceptualizing conceptualization, which focuses on the perfection of the momentary, which is not determined or limited by external seals or by the specific objects of the guru’s instructions. Otherwise, if the Blessed One intended the empowerment to be the washing away of stains, as in the passage "By this, one is washed and empowered," then how could it be [the case] merely by the instruction of the teaching?
U09: If one asks, "If the *tattva* (reality) possessing the nature of the tetralemma is taught, how could it not be that?"—that is indeed true. However, because *tattva* is not the domain of speech, it cannot be expressed, and it cannot be established through hearing. Regarding that, it is said:
U10: That which is luminous within is the direct hearing and seeing of speech; however, it is also that from which a reflection of conceptualization arises regarding sound.
U11: If one’s own conceptualization increases through that, and by meditating on the suchness of what has been conceptualized, conceptualization becomes disturbed in the mental continuum, then how could that be considered the realization of suchness? Thus it is taught.
U12: If, through empowerment, one were to realize *tattva* (reality) directly and all stains were to be purified without exception, then why would the *siddhi* (attainment) of *mahāmudrā* not be accomplished in that very instant? It is accomplished only by those with sharp faculties and blazing diligence. Does the fact that something is not desired or seen by anyone mean that it does not exist?
