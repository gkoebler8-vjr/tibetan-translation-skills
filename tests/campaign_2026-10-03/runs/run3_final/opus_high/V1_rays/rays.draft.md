### U01
For his own sake, as the dharmakaya, he abides in the state where samsara and nirvana are equal. This dharmakaya is unchanging. It is the element pure by nature, in which the adventitious stains have also been fully stilled. It is not conditioned by causes and conditions, and like space it is free from elaboration.

### U02
He possesses qualities such as the ten powers and the four fearlessnesses.

### U03
For the sake of others, as the sambhogakaya endowed with the five certainties, he appears as the foremost guide of the retinues of bodhisattva regents.

### U04
From the cloud of his great and loving compassion, as the supreme nirmanakaya, he pervades the realm of beings without partiality.

### U05
Displaying the twelve deeds, with the rain of the sublime dharma of peace and happiness he moistens the mindstreams of disciples whose potential has truly awakened, and so liberates them. I bow with respect in body, speech and mind to him, Shakyasimha, chief of the sugatas.

### U06
In this regard, it is said in the *Sublime Continuum*:
The Knower of the World, in great compassion,
looks upon the whole of the world;
then, never moving from the dharmakaya,
through emanations of many kinds

### U07
he shows the deeds of taking birth,
of passing from the Tushita heaven,
entering the womb, and being born,
delighting in the company of queens,

### U08
leaving home, practicing austerities,
going to the seat of enlightenment,
defeating the armies of mara,
attaining perfect enlightenment,
turning the wheel of dharma,

### U09
and passing into nirvana,
in realms that are not pure,
for as long as existence lasts.

### U10
Second. The Supreme Sage triumphed over the four maras: the aggregates, the Lord of Death, the son of the gods and the afflictions. His scriptures are free from the extremes of eternalism and nihilism, and as immeasurable as the depths of the ocean. This treatise has been condensed from the sutras and tantras among them, from the treatises of Nagarjuna, Asanga and others, and from the meaning of the profound pith instructions of the great Dharma Lord Drikungpa. Those instructions deserve to be honored like the jewel on the naga king's crown. In this condensation there is no stain of not understanding, of misunderstanding or of doubt. Just as every need and wish is fulfilled when a prayer is made to the vaidurya, the finest of jewels, so this treatise accomplishes every aim, temporary and ultimate. I shall explain it — the essence of the teachings, set out as three: ground, path and fruit.

### U11
Now, of the four points that begin with the purpose, the subject matter is the three: ground, path and fruit.

### U12
The purpose is to understand them through study and reflection. The ultimate purpose is that, through meditation on their meaning, the fruit becomes manifest. The connection is this: relying on the one before, each later one is connected to it step by step, as means and what arises from the means.
