### U01
HEADER: U01 · prose, opening clause of the homage period (U01–U05 one Tibetan sentence; English split into four) · homage, elevated · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
For his own sake, as the dharmakaya, he abides in the state where samsara and nirvana are equal. This dharmakaya is unchanging. It is the element pure by nature, in which the adventitious stains have also been fully stilled. It is not conditioned by causes and conditions, and like space it is free from elaboration.
NOTES:
Q: la after rnam par dag pa read as additive (twofold purity: pure by nature, and freed of adventitious stains); alt. locative, "the naturally pure element, in which the adventitious stains are stilled" (MITRA reads it locatively).
Q: nam mkha' ltar taken with "free from elaboration"; alt. with the whole description ("abides, like space, in ...").
Issue: the subject (Shakyasimha) is held back until U05 as in the Tibetan; the English uses "he" from the first sentence.
Issue: Tibetan attribute chain recast as three short predications after the main clause (surface only).

### U05
HEADER: U05 · prose, main verb of the homage period, first person · homage, elevated · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
Displaying the twelve deeds, with the rain of the sublime dharma of peace and happiness he moistens the mindstreams of disciples whose potential has truly awakened, and so liberates them. I bow with respect in body, speech and mind to him, Shakyasimha, chief of the sugatas.
NOTES:

Q: capitalization of dharma (lowercase here as a common noun per the mode); the editor may prefer Dharma throughout.
Issue: bstan te rendered as a participle ("Displaying the twelve deeds"); te kept as sequence/manner into the moistening.

### U06
HEADER: U06 · frame + verse: citation mode, rising, 3–4 beats; padas 7 × 4, four English lines; block U06–U09 order 1–15 with ston (9.3) and mdzad rnams (9.1) brought forward to line 5 · treatise verse, framed citation · seasoned practitioner · Toh 4024, Sublime Continuum (Mahayanottaratantrashastra) II.53, D 64b; verbatim canonical (dm.py identify)
TEXT:
In this regard, it is said in the *Sublime Continuum*:
The Knower of the World, in great compassion,
looks upon the whole of the world;
then, never moving from the dharmakaya,
through emanations of many kinds
NOTES:
Source: Sublime Continuum II.53–56 (Toh 4024, D 64b), verbatim; also in the vyakhya (Toh 4025). Register row: opening words "The Knower of the World, in great compassion".
Q: English short title fixed as *Sublime Continuum* (glossary TITLE row); the house may prefer *Uttaratantra*.
Q: 'jig rten mkhyen read as the epithet lokavid, "the Knower of the World", subject of the block; alt. predicate "with great compassion he knows the world" (MITRA's reading).
Q: frame formula "In this regard, it is said in ..." carries de yang; the bare formula "In the *Sublime Continuum* it is said:" would drop it.
Alt: sprul pa'i rang bzhin sna tshogs kyis, "through emanations of many kinds"; rang bzhin (nature) absorbed.
Check: "of every kind" changed to "of many kinds" (sna tshogs); "and" changed to "then" to keep the nas sequence.

### U07

### U10
HEADER: U10 · prose, one long Tibetan period split into eight English sentences · exposition (commitment to explain), elevated by simile, first person · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
Second. The Supreme Sage triumphed over the four maras: the aggregates, the Lord of Death, the son of the gods and the afflictions. His scriptures are free from the extremes of eternalism and nihilism, and as immeasurable as the depths of the ocean. This treatise has been condensed from the sutras and tantras among them, from the treatises of Nagarjuna, Asanga and others, and from the meaning of the profound pith instructions of the great Dharma Lord Drikungpa. Those instructions deserve to be honored like the jewel on the naga king's crown. In this condensation there is no stain of not understanding, of misunderstanding or of doubt. Just as every need and wish is fulfilled when a prayer is made to the vaidurya, the finest of jewels, so this treatise accomplishes every aim, temporary and ultimate. I shall explain it — the essence of the teachings, set out as three: ground, path and fruit.
NOTES:
Q: head of the period read as apposition, "it — the essence of the teachings, set out as three", echoing the root title; alt. genitive, "the essence of this treatise" (MITRA's reading).
Q: bkur 'os pa'i taken with Drikungpa's instructions only; alt. with all three sources.
Q: the first las read as partitive (the sutras and tantras among the Sage's scriptures); alt. a separate fourth source.
Q: chos rje 'bri gung pa chen po rendered "the great Dharma Lord Drikungpa"; the edition may prefer Jigten Sumgön.
Q: gnyis pa ni rendered "Second."; the editor may set the outline heading as a heading.
Alt: baidurya kept as "vaidurya"; alt. "beryl" or "lapis lazuli".
Issue: the agent of bsdus pa (the root author, Zhedang Dorje) is elided in the Tibetan and left unstated.
Check: "The treatise is free from the stains" changed to "In this condensation there is no stain" (med par is the manner of the condensing); "as three" restored for gsum du.
