### U01
HEADER: U01 · prose · exposition (the Buddha to a bodhisattva) · new practitioner · not a citation (Toh 147, ch. 2, the sutra's own text)
TEXT:
Furthermore, noble son, the Tathagata's aspiration never declines. What is aspiration here? It is aspiration for virtuous qualities. And what is that? The Tathagata's aspiration for great love never declines. The Tathagata's aspiration for great compassion never declines. His aspiration to teach the Dharma never declines. His aspiration to guide beings never declines. His aspiration to bring beings to maturity never declines. His aspiration for solitude never declines. His aspiration to lead beings to take up awakening properly never declines. His aspiration to keep the line of the Three Jewels unbroken never declines.

Q: rab tu dben pa rendered "solitude"; alt "complete seclusion".
Q: rigs rgyun rendered "line"; alt "lineage", "continuity".
Issue: The repeated "never declines" is kept in full; the house style does not license compressing stock repetition.
Issue: 'dul ba rendered "guide" (glossary), not "tame" or "discipline".
Check: in-context; no findings in this unit.

### U05
HEADER: U05 · prose · exposition (the Buddha to a bodhisattva) · new practitioner · not a citation (Toh 147, ch. 2, the sutra's own text)
TEXT:
Furthermore, noble son, the Tathagata's diligence never declines. Here the Tathagata's diligence is this: diligence that never neglects beings who are to be guided, and diligence that holds no contempt for those who listen to the Dharma. If the Tathagata finds a vessel for the Dharma, a listener who would not tire of hearing the Dharma even for an eon, then the Tathagata in turn will teach the Dharma. Even if his body went without food, he would not get up from his seat for an eon. Out of regard for beings, the Tathagata will travel across as many buddha fields as there are grains of sand in the Ganges, even for the sake of a single being to be guided by a buddha, wherever that being may be. And the Tathagata's body, speech and mind do not grow tired.
NOTES:
Q: "even for an eon" attaches to the listener's not tiring; alt to the length of the teaching.
Q: kha zas dang bral ba'i rgyud: rgyud read as "his body"; alt "his constitution".
Q: mi ldang: "from his seat" supplied with "get up" (ldang = rise).
Q: gang na: "wherever that being may be"; alt "where there is such a being".
Issue: The honorifics (sku, gsung, thugs) are carried by register, not by added adjectives.
Check: in-context; no findings.
MITRA: phrasing only (joins the last clause with "yet"; kept "And", since the Tibetan seam is neutral).

### U09
HEADER: U09 · prose, closing with the framing formula · exposition → framing · new practitioner · not a citation (Toh 147, ch. 2, the sutra's own text)
TEXT:
Furthermore, noble son, the Tathagata's mindfulness never declines in any way at all. Why is that? Because the Tathagata has no forgetfulness. Noble son, as soon as the Tathagata has fully awakened to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, he surveys the mindstreams of beings past, future and present. Even so, he forgets nothing whatever about them. Although he knows the conduct of beings exactly as it is, the Tathagata does not even direct his wisdom toward it. The Tathagata arranges beings into three groups, enters into their faculties, enters into their inclinations and surveys their conduct, and because of this his mindfulness never declines. The Tathagata does not first call things to mind, think them over or examine them and then teach beings the Dharma; and yet, when he teaches, nothing lapses. Why is that? Because the Tathagata's mindfulness never declines in any way. Just as he himself remembers and does not forget, so he teaches beings the Dharma. This is the twenty-third tathagata activity of the Tathagata. On this it is said:
NOTES:
Q: ma thag tu: "as soon as he has fully awakened ... he surveys"; alt a past narrative ("he surveyed").
Q: ye shes yang gtong ba med: "does not even direct his wisdom toward it"; alt "does not set wisdom aside in this either".
Q: phung po gsum: "three groups" of beings (traditionally those certain of the right, those certain of the wrong, and the undetermined; not glossed in the body); alt "three aggregates".
Q: The instrumental on the four coordinated actions rendered "because of this"; alt "through".
Q: ston kyang nyams pa med: "nothing lapses"; alt "his mindfulness does not lapse".
Q: ji ltar nyams pa med: "never declines in any way"; alt "stays undiminished, just as it is".
Check: in-context; no findings.
MITRA: differs at ye shes gtong ba ("does not have to exert his wisdom"); changed from "does not set wisdom aside" (yang = "even" supports it), Q recorded; reads phung po gsum as "three aggregates", kept "groups".
