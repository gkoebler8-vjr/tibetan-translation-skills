# sutra — Pass 2 draft (faithful)

### U01
Noble son, furthermore, the Tathagata's aspiration never declines. What is aspiration here? It is aspiration for virtuous qualities. And what is that? The Tathagata's aspiration for great love never declines. The Tathagata's aspiration for great compassion never declines. His aspiration to teach the Dharma never declines. His aspiration to guide beings never declines. His aspiration to bring beings to maturity never declines. His aspiration for solitude never declines. His aspiration to lead beings to take up awakening properly never declines. His aspiration to keep the line of the Three Jewels unbroken never declines.

### U02
The Tathagata does not act out of desire; rather, the Tathagata's aspiration is preceded by wisdom. That is why the Tathagata's aspiration is said never to decline. He also teaches the Dharma in order to bring unsurpassed aspiration to completion in all beings. This is the twenty-first tathagata activity of the Tathagata. On this it is said:

### U03
The Victor's aspiration always longs for and delights in the way of virtuous qualities.
To liberate beings, he also gives the Dharma of love and compassion.
His devotion to bringing beings to maturity and to the delights of the forest never declines.
He leads a great many to take up awakening and keeps the line of the Three Jewels unbroken.

### U04
He does not act out of desire, hatred, fear or delusion.
Having become exceptional, the wise one comes to realize all conditioned things through knowledge.
When the Victor sees beings who lack aspiration and are lazy, he brings them to aspiration.
With peerless knowledge he knows beings' acts of aspiration.

### U05
Noble son, furthermore, the Tathagata's diligence never declines. Here the Tathagata's diligence is this: diligence that never neglects beings who are to be guided, and diligence that holds no contempt for those who listen to the Dharma. If the Tathagata finds a vessel for the Dharma, a listener who would not tire of hearing the Dharma even for an eon, then the Tathagata in turn will teach the Dharma. Even if his body went without food, he would not get up from his seat for an eon. Out of regard for beings, the Tathagata will travel across as many buddha fields as there are grains of sand in the Ganges, even for the sake of a single being to be guided by a buddha, wherever that being may be; and the Tathagata's body, speech and mind do not grow tired.

### U06
The Tathagata's mind is thoroughly trained; he has taken up diligence and speaks in praise of diligence. He commends to beings the diligence of right application, the diligence through which beings will attain noble liberation. This is the twenty-second tathagata activity of the Tathagata. On this it is said:

### U07
He always speaks in praise of the power of the diligence
through which the Lion among Humans became exalted.
The power of diligence never declines in any way,
and finding those who are vessels, he also teaches the Dharma.

### U08
The Sugata's diligence never comes to rest;
in body, speech and mind he is never weary at all.
Just as self-arisen diligence is wholly free of wrongdoing,
so he trains the minds of beings and leads them to hold to what is right.

### U09
Noble son, furthermore, the Tathagata's mindfulness never declines in any way at all. Why is that? Because the Tathagata has no forgetfulness. Noble son, as soon as the Tathagata has fully awakened to unsurpassed, perfect and complete awakening, he surveys the mindstreams of beings past, future and present, and yet he forgets nothing whatever about them. Although he knows the conduct of beings exactly as it is, in this too the Tathagata does not set wisdom aside. The Tathagata arranges beings into three groups, enters into their faculties, enters into their inclinations and surveys their conduct, and because of this his mindfulness never declines. The Tathagata does not first call things to mind, think them over or examine them and then teach beings the Dharma; and yet, when he teaches, nothing lapses. Why is that? Because the Tathagata's mindfulness never declines in any way. Just as he himself remembers and does not forget, so he teaches beings the Dharma. This is the twenty-third tathagata activity of the Tathagata. On this it is said:

### U10
The Teacher's mindfulness never declines at all;
the Victor's mindfulness has nothing it needs to call to mind.
From the moment the Victor attained awakening and became a buddha,
he has known the minds of beings without exception.

### U11
There is nothing he must come to know again later
about the inclinations, faculties and conduct of beings.
Abiding in effortless activity,
the Lord of Victors teaches beings the Dharma.
