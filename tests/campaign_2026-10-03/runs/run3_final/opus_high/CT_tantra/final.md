### U01
HEADER: U01 · verse: citation mode, rising, 3–4 beats; padas 7 x 4, four lines, order 1-2-3-4 · instruction · seasoned practitioner · Toh 431, Candamaharosana Tantra, ch. 15; not a citation
TEXT:
Through that union the yogi swiftly
gains siddhi, without a doubt.
Or else he should cultivate
a deep equipoise without a prajna.
NOTES:
Q: de yi sbyor ba: "that union" (with the consort of the preceding verses) or "that practice"; MITRA reads "the practice of that".
Q: shes rab taken as prajna, the female partner, per the tantra's usage; alternative "an equipoise devoid of wisdom" (MITRA's reading). Kept.
Q: by grammar "without a prajna" qualifies the equipoise; in sense it is the yogi who has no partner.
Alt: "a deep equipoise" renders shin tu mnyam gzhag ("a very settled equipoise").

### U05
HEADER: U05 · prose · exposition (symbolic equations) · seasoned practitioner · Toh 431, Candamaharosana Tantra, ch. 15; not a citation
TEXT:
Here, the square is the four immeasurables. The four doors are the four truths. The four archways are the four dhyanas. The eight pillars are the eightfold noble path. The single tier is one-pointedness of mind. The lotus is the womb. The various colors are the various emanations. The nine deities are the nine branches of scripture. The red directions are great passion. The white, yellow, green, and black in the intermediate directions are the brahmins, vaishyas, kshatriyas, and shudras themselves. The moon and sun are semen and sweat. The sword in the center is the emblem of Black Acala. The curved knife is Dveshavajri. The directions beginning with the east are White Acala and the others. The intermediate directions beginning with the southeast belong to Mohavajri and the others. This is the purity of the mandala.
NOTES:
Q: 'phar ma gcig pa rendered "single tier"; the architectural term is uncertain (MITRA: "single projection").
Q: phyogs dmar po = "the red directions" or "the red of the directions".
Q: kha dog sna tshogs pa may be the colors of the lotus just named, or of the mandala.
Q: rngul is "sweat" where one might expect the female fluid; rendered as written.
Alt: "beginning with the southeast" renders me la sogs pa, the fire quarter.
Issue: da mangs rigs read as dmangs rigs (shudra).
Check: in-context; no findings.

### U06
HEADER: U06 · prose · exposition · seasoned practitioner · Toh 431, Candamaharosana Tantra, ch. 15; not a citation
TEXT:
I shall state the purity of the meditation. First, the offering is the exceptional act of the accumulation of merit. Emptiness is the accumulation of wisdom, an exceptional death; it is the stainless body, that is, the body of the gandharva. The culmination of the celestial palace is the level of buddhahood. The lotus is the womb. The moon and sun are the white and the red.
NOTES:
Q: khyad par 'phags pa'i may qualify the accumulation ("the exceptional accumulation of merit") rather than the act.
Q: 'chi ba khyad par 'phags pa read as "an exceptional death"; alternative "death; the exceptional, stainless body".
Q: gzhal yas khang gi mthar thug pa read as a genitive ("the culmination of the celestial palace"); alternative, gi for ni: "the celestial palace is the ultimate level of buddhahood".
Check: in-context; 2 minor: "accumulating merit" restored to the term "accumulation of merit"; "in its culmination" brought back to the construal's genitive.

### U07
HEADER: U07 · prose · exposition (symbolic narrative of conception) · seasoned practitioner · Toh 431, Candamaharosana Tantra, ch. 15; not a citation
TEXT:
The syllable HUM is the mind of the gandharva between the mother and the father. Akshobhya is the father; Mamaki is the mother. Seeing their passion for each other, the gandharva feels hatred toward the father and, attached to the mother, enters through delusion just as sentient beings do. The son who has entered the lotus kills the father and then attains his rank. Having taken the mother, through the exceptional bliss of the latent tendencies of endless births, he too is born as a son. The daughters, again, are White Acala and the others, and also Mohavajri and the others. The sons too, having killed the father, go to another; thinking, "This is the enemy," they kill him. The daughters too, through the exceptional bliss of the latent tendencies of endless births, feel desire.
NOTES:
Q: de yang bur skye bar byed read as "he too is born as a son" (changed after MITRA; bur = "as a son", byed auxiliary); alternative causative "brings about the birth of a son".
Q: bu mo "daughters" is paired with White Acala; the text may intend "sons" or "children"; rendered as written.
Q: gzhan la bgrod = "go to another", probably sexual approach; "him" in "they kill him" may be the father or the other.
Issue: "the gandharva" supplies the elided subject of "feels hatred" and "enters" (MITRA: generic "one").
Check: in-context; 1 major ("kills the father and so attains": nas sequence read as cause; restored as "and then"); 1 minor ("any" removed).

### U08
HEADER: U08 · prose · exposition (iconographic symbolism) · seasoned practitioner · Toh 431, Candamaharosana Tantra, ch. 15; not a citation
TEXT:
The sword is wisdom, and the noose is method. Alternatively, the noose is wisdom and the sword is method. That the two have become one taste is the threatening forefinger. The left eye looking down protects the seven underworlds. The right eye looking up protects the seven eggs of Brahma. The left knee planted on the ground protects the earth. Stamping down with the right foot frightens all the maras. The mara of the aggregates is Brahma, the mara of the afflictions is Ishvara, the mara of the Lord of Death is Vishnu, and the mara of the son of the gods is Indra.
NOTES:
Q: the head noun of "the left looking down / the right looking up" is elided; "eye" is supplied; MITRA reads "looking downward to the left".
Issue: "ses rab" in the Tibetan read as shes rab; shes rab here = wisdom (wisdom–method pair), not prajna in the U01 sense.
Alt: yang dag par bsnun pa = "stamping down" (firmly).
Check: in-context; 1 minor (read-aloud repair of "That the two have become one taste").
