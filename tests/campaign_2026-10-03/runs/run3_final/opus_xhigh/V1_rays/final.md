### U01
HEADER: U01 · prose: homage period, first of five units forming one Tibetan sentence (main verb in U05) · homage, elevated · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
For his own benefit he is the dharmakaya, which neither moves nor changes. In the element, pure by nature, the adventitious stains are completely pacified. Unconditioned by causes and conditions and free of elaboration, he abides like space in the equality of samsara and nirvana,
NOTES:
Issue: U01–U05 are one Tibetan sentence whose head, Shakyasimha, is named only at its close; the English runs the description as "he" sentences resolved by "To him, Shakyasimha" in U05, and keeps the pairing "for his own benefit" (dharmakaya) / "for the benefit of others" (form bodies, U03–U05).
Q: chos sku 'pho 'gyur med pa — taken as "he is the dharmakaya"; alt. "his dharmakaya, which neither moves nor changes, abides …" (same sense).
Q: khams rang bzhin gyis rnam par dag pa la — la taken as locus (in the element pure by nature the stains are pacified: the twofold purity); alt. connective "the element is pure by nature, and the adventitious stains are pacified".
Q: 'dus ma byas pa spros pa dang bral ba — taken as predicates of the dharmakaya; alt. as describing space ("like space, unconditioned and free of elaboration").
Alt: "completely pacified" — "completely dispelled"; "free of elaboration" — "beyond conceptual elaboration".
Check: in-context; ngang ("state") absorbed into "in the equality" (kept); glossary aligned to "neither moves nor changes". MITRA: no material difference.

### U05
HEADER: U05 · prose: close of the homage period, its one finite verb ('dud do) · homage, elevated; the speaker's own bow in first person · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
He shows the twelve deeds; then, with the rain of the sacred Dharma of peace and happiness, he moistens the mindstreams of disciples whose potential has fully awakened, and so sets them free. To him, Shakyasimha, chief of the sugatas, I bow with respect in body, speech and mind.
NOTES:
Q: rigs yang dag par sad pa'i — "whose potential has fully awakened"; alt. "whose potential he has properly awakened".
Q: zhi bde dam pa'i chos — "the sacred Dharma of peace and happiness"; alt. apposition ("peace and happiness, the sacred Dharma").
Q: mdzad pa bcu gnyis su bstan te — "shows the twelve deeds"; alt. "teaches by means of the twelve deeds".
Alt: "Shakyasimha" — "the Lion of the Shakyas"; "disciples" — "those to be trained"; "sets them free" — "liberates them".
Check: in-context, 2 minor applied — te restored as "then" (had become "and"); "To him" added so the relative chain of U01–U05 lands on its head.
Q: MITRA hangs "having revealed the twelve deeds … having moistened" on "I bow" (speaker as agent); kept: grol bar mdzad pa'i is a relative clause (honorific mdzad) on Shakyasimha, so the Buddha is the agent.

### U10
HEADER: U10 · prose: one long period (sa bcad "second"), split into seven English sentences · exposition with two similes; first person at the close · seasoned practitioner · not a citation
TEXT:
Second: The supreme Sage has conquered the four maras — the aggregates, the lord of death, the son of the gods and the afflictions. His scriptures are free of the extremes of permanence and annihilation, and as immeasurable as the ocean's expanse. This text draws on the sutras and tantras among them, on the treatises of Nagarjuna, Asanga and others, and on the meaning of the profound pith instructions of the great Drikungpa, Lord of Dharma, which deserve veneration like the jewel on a naga king's crown. It condenses them without the stains of not understanding, misunderstanding and doubt. Just as everything one needs and wishes for comes forth when one prays to the beryl, supreme among jewels, so it is a treatise that accomplishes every aim, temporary and ultimate. It is the Essence, set out in three parts — ground, path and fruition — and I shall explain it.
NOTES:
Q: head noun — bstan bcos … gzhi dang lam dang 'bras bu gsum du bstan pa'i snying po taken as apposition (the treatise, which is the Essence set out in three parts); alt. genitive "the essence of the treatise" (MITRA "the heart of the treatise"); kept: no genitive particle follows bstan bcos.
Q: klu rgyal gyi gtsug gi nor bu ltar bkur 'os pa'i — taken as modifying Drikungpa's instructions; alt. all the listed sources.
Q: gsung rab … las mdo sde dang rgyud sde rnams — partitive ("the sutras and tantras among them"); alt. apposition ("his scriptures, the sutras and tantras"); MITRA coordinates them with the scriptures.
Q: gnyis pa ni — an outline heading (the second part of the preamble, the commitment to explain), rendered "Second:"; the edition may set it as a heading.
Q: "the great Drikungpa, Lord of Dharma" is Jigten Sumgön (1143–1217); a name footnote is the editor's call.
Issue: "the Essence" capitalized as the short name of the root text, since bstan pa'i snying po echoes its title, theg chen bstan pa'i snying po.
Alt: "beryl" — "vaidurya"; "not understanding, misunderstanding and doubt" — "incomprehension, misunderstanding and doubt".
Check: in-context, 3 minor kept — "draws on" (a verb supplied at the split, restating "from within"), "condenses" (present for past bsdus, with the text as agent).
