# shastra — draft (Pass 2 + Pass 3, academic; revised after check)

### U01
On "the divisions of *saṃvara* and so on": *saṃvara* means consecration (*abhiṣeka*). Its subdivision is [what the root calls] "division," and its characteristics will be explained [below]. As it is said:

### U02
For the benefit of beings
consecration was proclaimed as fourfold.
Because by this one is washed and consecrated,
it is therefore called "consecration."

So it was said.

### U03
In order to state the division of those very four consecrations, [the root] says as follows:

### U04
The master, the secret, wisdom,
and that, thus, the fourth again—

With these words and the rest, [the division] will be explained.

### U05
Here in this yoginī tantra, "master" (*ācārya*) [is so called] because, having become remote from evil, nonvirtuous things, he conducts himself thoroughly (*kun du spyod pa*, ā-√car); for that very [consecration] is his *saṃvara*. The nature of the master consecration is what is called the full actualization, from the karmamudrā and by means of the four moments, of the very own-nature of the four joys. It is not, however, to be taken as "being consecrated with the master consecration through having obtained the irreversible consecration," because that is shared with the kriyā tantras and the rest. Therefore the statement "[by it] one also comes to possess the fortune to explain, hear, and so on, all the yoga and yoginī tantras and the rest" amounts to nothing. Therefore, first of all, in order to have authority to hear, meditate on, and so on, the yoginī tantras such as the *Hevajra*, one should be consecrated with the master consecration itself. Moreover, it should be understood that [consecration] is indicated by the characteristics of master, secret, wisdom, and fourth, as in the *abhisamaya*.

### U06
To those of dull faculties who have obtained such a master consecration, having secured them through conviction, one should teach the meditation on the karmamudrā and on the mudrā with elaboration (*prapañca*). Likewise, on the side of the completion stage as well, the experience—through the guru's instruction itself—of the four joys, whose nature is the four moments, at the jewel residing in the secret [place] is called the secret consecration; for it is not to be taught to yogins engaged in meditation with elaboration. Here, to consecrated [disciples] of middling faculties, one should teach and explain the meditation on the samayamudrā.

### U07
Likewise, the special gnosis is wisdom (*prajñā*), that is, the knowledge that all phenomena are mind only. The consecration [given] to actualize it is the wisdom-gnosis consecration. Moreover, what the guru's instruction itself correctly indicates—the external mudrā and the moments—in the heart-jewel, an embodied form condensing the nature of the three consciousnesses, the dependent, the imagined, and the transformed, should be understood as wisdom-gnosis. To those of superior faculties who have been consecrated with that consecration, one should teach and explain the dharmamudrā by means of the illusion-like samādhi.

### U08
Likewise there is also the fourth: "that, thus, … again." "That" is wisdom-gnosis. "Thus" (*de bzhin*) is thusness (*tathatā*): there is no difference in meaning among [thusness], the real limit (*bhūtakoṭi*), and the dharmadhātu. The consecration by which one sees or actualizes its nature is what the word "thus" expresses. The word "again" means: "it is to be given again, immediately after the wisdom-gnosis consecration." Moreover, as for that [fourth]: with the external mudrā, and through the guru's instruction itself, one focuses, by the yoga of focusing without a reference point, on the perfection of the moment, which is characterized as not being definitely delimited into separate objects. Merely having taught the instruction on what is to be called "the fourth consecration" does not make it the fourth consecration. Otherwise—since, in the words "With this one is washed and consecrated" and so on, the Blessed One holds that consecration here is the washing away of stains—how could it come about by merely teaching an instruction?

### U09
Suppose it is objected: "If reality, whose nature is the fourth, is taught, how would [that teaching] not become that [fourth consecration]?" That is true. However, because reality is not within the range of speech, it cannot be expressed, nor can it be accomplished through hearing. As it is said:

### U10
That reality, clear within,
is heard and seen directly from speech;
yet in sound there arises
a reflection of conceptual thought.

### U11
One's own conceptual thought expands it;
if, by meditating on an imagined reality,
conceptual thought is stirred up in the mind-stream,
how will reality be realized?

So it is taught.

### U12
