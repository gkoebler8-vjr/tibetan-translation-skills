### U01
HEADER: U01 · verse: citation mode, rising, 3–4 beats; padas 7 × 4, four lines, order 1-2-3-4 · instruction · seasoned practitioner · not a citation (root text, Toh 431)
TEXT:
The yogin, through union with her,
gains siddhi swiftly, beyond doubt.
Or else, without a prajna,
cultivate profound equipoise.
NOTES:
Q: "her" is taken as the consort of the preceding verses (outside this span); it could be "that practice".
Q: "without a prajna" is taken to describe the practitioner (one without a consort); it may instead qualify the equipoise ("an equipoise free of prajna"), which is how MITRA reads it.
Issue: prajna here is the wisdom partner; kept as the tradition's term, distinct from "wisdom" (ye shes).
Check: in-context; no findings. MITRA differs (equipoise devoid of prajna); kept, see Q.

### U05
HEADER: U05 · prose · exposition (symbolic correspondences) · seasoned practitioner · not a citation (root text, Toh 431)
TEXT:
Here, the square is the four immeasurable abodes. The four doors are the four truths. The four archways are the four dhyanas. The eight pillars are the noble eightfold path. The single tier is one-pointedness of mind. The lotus is the place of birth. The various colors are the various emanations. The nine deities are the nine branches of scripture. The red quarters are great passion. The white, yellow, green and black in the intermediate directions are the brahmin, vaishya, kshatriya and shudra castes. The moon and sun are semen and sweat. The sword in the center is the emblem of Black Acala. The flaying knife is Dveshavajri. The directions beginning with the east are White Acala and the others. The intermediate directions beginning with the southeast belong to Mohavajri and the others. This is the purification of the mandala.
NOTES:
Q: 'phar ma gcig pa ("the single tier") names an architectural member of the palace that is uncertain (ledge, projection, tier).
Q: phyogs dmar po may be singular, "the red direction" (MITRA so reads it).
Q: rngul is literally "sweat"; it probably stands for the female generative fluid, kept literal.
Alt: Dveshavajri and Mohavajri are the Sanskrit names of zhe sdang rdo rje ma and gti mug rdo rje ma ("Hatred Vajri", "Delusion Vajri").
Issue: "southeast" renders me, the fire quarter.
Check: in-context; 1 minor (gnas, "abodes", omitted), restored.

### U06
HEADER: U06 · prose · exposition · seasoned practitioner · not a citation (root text, Toh 431)
TEXT:
I shall now state the purification of the meditation. First, the offering is the supreme action of the accumulation of merit. Emptiness is the accumulation of wisdom: the supreme death, the stainless body, that is, the body of the gandharva. The culmination of the palace is the level of buddhahood. The lotus is the place of birth. The moon and sun are the white and the red.
NOTES:
Q: "supreme" may qualify the accumulation ("the action of the supreme accumulation of merit").
Q: the emptiness sentence may be two equations (emptiness = accumulation of wisdom and the supreme death; the stainless body = the gandharva's body); kept as one appositive series.
Q: "the culmination of the palace" vs "the palace, the ultimate, is the level of buddhahood".
Issue: gandharva is the being of the intermediate state; the text spells the palace gzhal yang khang (for gzhal yas khang).
Check: in-context; no findings.

### U07
HEADER: U07 · prose · exposition (conception as symbol) · seasoned practitioner · not a citation (root text, Toh 431)
TEXT:
The syllable HUM is the mind of the gandharva between the mother and the father. Akshobhya is the father; Mamaki is the mother. Seeing them in passion with each other, the gandharva feels hatred toward the father and desire for the mother, then, through delusion, enters, just as an ordinary sentient being does. The son who has entered the lotus kills the father and takes his place. Having taken the mother, through the supreme bliss of the habitual tendencies of beginningless births, he in turn brings about birth as a son. The daughters, again, are White Acala and the others, and Mohavajri and the others as well. The sons too kill the father and then go on to others; thinking, "This one is the enemy," they kill him. The daughters too, through the supreme bliss of the habitual tendencies of beginningless births, feel desire.
NOTES:
Q: de yang bur skye bar byed: the object is unclear ("causes that one too to be born as a son"; MITRA: "he is born as a son"); rendered "he in turn brings about birth as a son".
Q: the text assigns both White Acala and Mohavajri to "the daughters"; one would expect the Acalas as sons; rendered as the text stands.
Q: gzhan la bgrod pa: "go on to others" or "approach other women"; "this one" is taken as the father.
Issue: the text spells ma bskyod pa ni pa'o for mi bskyod pa ni pha'o; mtha' med "endless" rendered "beginningless" for past births.
Check: in-context; 1 minor ('thob go 'phang as "takes his place"), kept as idiom. MITRA differs at the son's birth; kept, see Q.

### U08
HEADER: U08 · prose · exposition (iconography) · seasoned practitioner · not a citation (root text, Toh 431)
TEXT:
The sword is prajna and the noose is method. Or else, the noose is prajna and the sword is method. The two becoming one taste is the threatening forefinger. The left gaze, turned downward, protects the seven underworlds. The right gaze, turned upward, protects the seven eggs of Brahma. The left knee planted on the ground protects the earth. Stamping firmly with the right foot terrifies all the maras. The mara of the aggregates is Brahma; the mara of the afflictions is Ishvara; the mara of the Lord of Death is Vishnu; the mara of the son of the gods is Indra.
NOTES:
Q: g.yon pa / g.yas pa "looking down / up" names no organ; taken as the deity's left and right eyes (his squinting gaze); MITRA reads "looking downward to the left".
Q: the text's bus mo is read as pus mo, "knee".
Issue: "the seven eggs of Brahma" are the Brahma worlds above, balancing the seven underworlds.
Check: in-context; 1 minor ("gaze" interprets bltas pa), kept with Q. MITRA differs at the gaze; kept.

### U09
HEADER: U09 · prose · exposition · seasoned practitioner · not a citation (root text, Toh 431)
TEXT:

The man born from semen and sweat is entity; the woman is non-entity. Black is consciousness, white is form, yellow is feeling, red is perception, and green is Amoghasiddhi.
NOTES:

Q: the subject of "dwells" is elided; taken as the deity.
Q: green is given as Amoghasiddhi where the aggregate of formations is expected; kept as the text has it.
