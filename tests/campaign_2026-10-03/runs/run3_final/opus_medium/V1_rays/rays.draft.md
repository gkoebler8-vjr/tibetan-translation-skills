# rays — draft after style pass (seasoned practitioner)
U01: For his own sake, his dharmakaya is unchanging. Its element is pure by nature, and the adventitious stains are completely stilled. It is unconditioned by causes and conditions and free from elaboration, and like space it abides in the equality of samsara and nirvana.
U02: It is endowed with qualities such as the ten powers and the four fearlessnesses.
U03: For the sake of others, his sambhogakaya, endowed with the five certainties, appears as the foremost guide of the retinues of bodhisattvas who are his regents.
U04: From the cloud of his loving great compassion, his supreme nirmanakaya pervades the realm of beings without partiality.
U05: He displays the twelve deeds, and with the rain of the sublime Dharma of peace and bliss he moistens the mindstreams of disciples whose potential has truly awakened, and so liberates them. To him, the Lion of the Shakyas, chief of the sugatas, I bow with respect in body, speech and mind.
U06: Accordingly, in the *Uttaratantra* it is said: / Through great compassion the Knower of the World, / having looked upon every world, / without moving from the dharmakaya, / through emanations of many kinds displays
U07: his former births and taking birth, / leaving his abode in Tushita, / entering the womb, being born, / delighting in play amid his consorts,
U08: renunciation, the practice of austerities, / going to the seat of enlightenment, / defeating the hosts of mara, / attaining perfect enlightenment, / turning the wheel of Dharma,
U09: and passing into nirvana — / he displays these deeds in impure realms / for as long as existence lasts.
U10: Second. The scriptures of the supreme Sage, who is victorious over the four maras — the aggregates, the Lord of Death, the son of the gods and the afflictions — are free from the extremes of permanence and annihilation, and immeasurable like the expanse of the ocean. Free from the stains of not understanding, misunderstanding and doubt, this text has been condensed from among them: from the sutras and the tantras, from the treatises of Nagarjuna, Asanga and others, and from the meaning of the profound pith instructions of the great Dharma Lord Drikungpa. That meaning deserves to be honored like the crest jewel of the naga king. If you pray to the vaidurya, supreme among jewels, every need and wish is fulfilled; just so, this treatise accomplishes every aim, temporary and ultimate. I shall explain this Essence, which teaches the ground, the path and the fruition.
U11: Now, of the four topics beginning with the purpose, the subject matter is the three: ground, path and fruition.
U12: The purpose is to understand this through study and reflection. The ultimate purpose is that, through meditation on its meaning, the fruition becomes manifest. The connection is this: relying on what comes before, what comes after follows in sequence; they are connected in the manner of a means and what arises from the means.
