### U01
TEXT:
On "the division of the saṃvara and so on": "saṃvara" means consecration (abhiṣeka). Its subdivision (prabheda) is the "division" (bheda), whose defining characteristics will be explained [below]. And this is as is said:
NOTES:
Q: Kāṇha glosses saṃvara as consecration; "saṃvara" is kept rather than translated as "vow" so that the gloss can stand.
Q: "the division of the saṃvara and so on" is taken as the quoted root lemma; its exact root wording is not checked here.

### U02
TEXT:
For the benefit of beings
consecration is proclaimed as fourfold.
Since by this one is washed and consecrated,
it is therefore called "consecration."
So it is stated.
NOTES:
Q: bkrus shing dbang bskur ("washed and consecrated") answers to the Sanskrit etymology sicyate snāpyate ("is sprinkled, is bathed"); "consecrated" in line 3 could be rendered "sprinkled" to bring out the nirukti.

### U04
TEXT:
Master, secret, and wisdom,
and the fourth is that, thus, again—
by this and what follows [the subdivision] will be explained.
NOTES:
Q: Line 2 (de ltar de bzhin bzhi pa yang) is rendered to fit Kāṇha's gloss in U08 ("that" = wisdom-gnosis, "thus" = thusness, "again" = conferred again afterwards); read without the commentary it means simply "and likewise the fourth as well."

### U05
TEXT:
In this Yoginī tantra one is a master (ācārya) because, having become far removed from sinful, non-virtuous dharmas, one practises [the conduct] thoroughly; [the master consecration is so called] because it is the saṃvara of that very [master]. The nature of the master consecration is what is called the full realization of the very own-nature of the four joys through the four moments, from the action seal (karmamudrā). It is not, however, being consecrated with the master consecration through having obtained the irreversible consecration (avaivartikābhiṣeka), since that is shared with the Kriyā tantras and the rest. The statement "Therefore one also becomes endowed with the fortune to expound, to hear, and so on, all [the tantras], the Yoga and Yoginī tantras and the rest" is of no account. Therefore, first of all, to authorize [the disciple] to hear, to meditate on, and so on, the Yoginī tantras such as the Hevajra, [the master] should consecrate [him] with the master consecration itself. And this, it should be understood, is distinguished by the defining marks of the master, the secret, the wisdom, and the fourth [consecrations], as [set out] in the abhisamaya.
NOTES:
Q: The etymology of ācārya plays on Sanskrit ārāt ("far off") and ā-carati ("practises fully"); the Tibetan does not show the pun, and "the conduct" is supplied.
Q: de nyid kyi de'i sdom pa yin pas so may instead mean "because that very [consecration] is its [the Yoginī tantra's] saṃvara."
Q: The sentence on the irreversible consecration is syntactically loose; alternative: "but one who has obtained the irreversible consecration, which is common to the Kriyā tantras and so on, is not [thereby] to be counted as consecrated with the master consecration."
Q: de lta bas na ("therefore") is taken as the opening of the rejected statement; it may instead be Kāṇha's own connective ("Hence the statement ... is of no account").
Q: mngon par rtogs pa ji lta ba bzhin du is taken as "as in the abhisamaya (the ritual manual)"; alternatively "according to [the disciple's] realization."

### U06
TEXT:
To those of dull faculties who have obtained such a master consecration, after first securing them through devotion (adhimukti), the cultivation of the action seal and of the elaborated seal is to be taught. Likewise, on the side of the completion stage as well, the experience of the four joys, whose nature is the four moments, at the jewel abiding in the secret [place], through the very instruction of the guru—this is called the secret consecration, since it is not to be taught to yogins engaged in the cultivation of elaboration. Here, to the consecrated of middling faculties, the cultivation of the pledge seal (samayamudrā) is to be taught and explained.
NOTES:
Q: mos pas nges par bzung nas, "after securing them through devotion"; alternatively "having confirmed [them] on account of their faith" or "restricting [them] according to their inclination".
Q: las kyi phyag rgya dang spros pa'i phyag rgya bsgom pa may also mean "the elaborate cultivation of the action seal" (one practice, not two).
Q: gsang bar gnas pa'i nor bu, "the jewel abiding in the secret [place]", is taken as the locus of the experience (cf. "heart-jewel" in U07); it could instead qualify the guru ("the guru, a jewel abiding in secrecy").

### U07
TEXT:
Likewise, distinguished gnosis is wisdom (prajñā), that is, the knowledge that all dharmas are mind only. The consecration for fully realizing it is the wisdom-gnosis consecration (prajñājñānābhiṣeka). Moreover, it should be understood that the wisdom-gnosis is this: in the heart-jewel, whose form gathers up the nature of the three consciousnesses—the dependent, the imagined, and the transformed—the outer seal and the moments are correctly indicated through the very instruction of the guru. To those of the highest faculties who are consecrated with that consecration, the dharma seal (dharmamudrā) is to be taught and explained by means of the illusion-like concentration.
NOTES:
Q: The first gloss may run the other way: "wisdom (prajñā) is distinguished gnosis", with prajñā as the root word glossed.
Q: yongs su gyur pa, "transformed" (parāvṛtta?), stands where the three-nature scheme has pariniṣpanna (yongs grub, "perfected"); perhaps a translator's variant or a different reading.
Q: snying gi nor bu, "heart-jewel", may mean the jewel at the heart [cakra] or the jewel that is the heart (essence); the identification is uncertain.

### U08
TEXT:
Likewise there is the fourth as well: [this is] "that, thus, again." "That" is the wisdom-gnosis. "Thus" is thusness (tathatā): there is no difference in meaning between it and [the terms] "reality-limit" (bhūtakoṭi), "dharma-sphere" (dharmadhātu) and the rest. The consecration by which one sees or fully realizes its nature is what the word "thus" is to express. The word "again" means "it is to be conferred again directly after the wisdom-gnosis consecration." Moreover, that [consecration] is this: with the outer seal, and through the very instruction of the guru, one apprehends, by the yoga of apprehending without an object of apprehension, the consummate moment, whose defining mark is that it is not delimited by determination to any particular object—that is to be called the fourth consecration. It does not become the fourth consecration merely through the teaching of an instruction. Otherwise, since by [the words] "Since by this one is washed and consecrated" and so on the Blessed One intends consecration here as the washing away of stains, how could that come about merely through the teaching of an instruction?
NOTES:
Q: The lemma here (de ltar de bzhin yang) differs slightly from the root as quoted in U04 (de ltar de bzhin bzhi pa yang); "that" is glossed from de, "thus" from de bzhin.
Q: The sentence on the fourth runs on in the Tibetan; alternative division: "it does not become the fourth consecration merely by teaching the instruction [on what] is to be called 'the fourth consecration'."
Q: skad cig ma'i phun sum tshogs pa, "the consummate moment" (kṣaṇasampad?), may instead mean "the excellence of the moments".

### U10
TEXT:
That reality, luminous within,
is heard and seen directly from words;
but in sound there comes to arise
the reflected image of conceptual thought.
NOTES:
Q: Line 2 (ngag las mngon sum thos mthong yin) is obscure: it may voice the supposition being refuted ("[were it] heard and seen directly from words"), or mean "is direct perception, [not] hearing and seeing [derived] from words"; the indicative is kept.

### U11
TEXT:
That [image] swells one's own conceptual thought;
through meditating on an imagined reality,
if the mind-stream is stirred with concepts,
how could reality be realized?
So it is taught.
NOTES:
Q: rang gi rnam rtog de de rgyas is read "that [reflected image] swells one's own conceptual thought"; alternatively "one's own concepts thereby proliferate".
Q: rtog par in line 4 is read as rtogs par ("realized"); as written it would mean "conceived" or "examined".

### U12

Does something become nonexistent because someone does not accept it and has not seen it? And this is as is said:
NOTES:
Q: sus kyang mi 'dod cing ma mthong bas is read as Sanskrit kenacit ("because someone does not accept or has not seen it"); it could also be "because no one at all accepts or has seen it".
