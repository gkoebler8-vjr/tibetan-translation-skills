### U01
TEXT:
To him who, for his own sake, abides as the dharmakaya, beyond transition and change — the element pure by its very nature, its adventitious stains fully stilled as well, unconditioned by causes and conditions, free from elaboration like space — in the state where samsara and nirvana are equal;
NOTES:
Q: The particle after "pure by nature" is read as additive, giving the two purities (natural purity, and purity from adventitious stains); it could also be read "in which the adventitious stains are stilled".
Q: "Like space" is attached to "free from elaboration"; it could equally qualify the abiding in equality.

### U05
TEXT:
displays the twelve deeds, and with the rain of the sublime Dharma of peace and bliss moistens the mindstreams of those to be trained whose spiritual potential has been truly awakened, and so sets them free — to the chief of all sugatas, the Lion of the Shakyas, I bow with reverence in body, speech and mind.
NOTES:
Q: Shakya Sengé rendered "the Lion of the Shakyas"; the editor may prefer the name form "Shakya Sengé" or "Shakyasimha".
Q: rigs rendered "spiritual potential" (the awakened buddha-family of the disciple).

### U06
TEXT:
As the Uttaratantra says:
The Knower of the World, who looks
in great compassion on all the world,
not stirring from the dharmakaya,
through emanations of every kind
NOTES:
Q: 'jig rten mkhyen taken as the epithet "Knower of the World" (lokavit), the subject of the whole stanza sequence; great compassion is moved to the looking, which is where the sense puts it.
Q: Title form: the editor may prefer "Sublime Continuum" or "Ratnagotravibhaga" to "Uttaratantra".

### U07
TEXT:
his former lives, his heavenly birth,
his passing from the Tushita realm,
conception in the womb, his birth,
his pleasures with his court of queens,
NOTES:
Q: skye ba mngon par skye ba read with the Sanskrit as "former lives (the jataka births) and his birth among the Tushita gods"; it could also be taken as one item, "manifestly taking birth".
Q: The Tibetan as given lacks the canonical pada bzo yi gnas la mkhas pa dang (skill in the arts) after "his birth", so only eleven of the twelve deeds appear. If the editor restores it, insert after line 3: "his mastery of arts and crafts,".

### U10
TEXT:
Second: From the scriptures of the Supreme Sage, victor over the four maras — the aggregates, the Lord of Death, the son of the gods and the afflictions — scriptures free from the extremes of permanence and annihilation and as immeasurable as the expanse of the ocean; from the sutras and the tantras among them, from the treatises of Nagarjuna, Asanga and others, and from the profound meaning of the pith instructions of the great Dharma Lord Drikungpa, worthy of honor like the jewel on the crown of the king of nagas — from within all these, this treatise has been gathered, free from the stains of incomprehension, misunderstanding and doubt. As everything one needs and wishes for comes forth when one prays to the vaidurya, supreme among jewels, so this treatise brings every aim, temporary and ultimate, to fulfillment. I shall now explain it — the Essence of the Teachings, set out as ground, path and fruition.
NOTES:
Q: "Second" is the outline marker for the second section, the commitment to explain; the editor may turn it into a heading or drop it.
Q: The one Tibetan period is divided into three English sentences for readability.
Q: "Worthy of honor like the jewel on the crown of the king of nagas" is read as qualifying Drikungpa's pith instructions; it may be meant to qualify all the sources listed.
Q: The great Dharma Lord Drikungpa is Jigten Sumgön.
Q: "This treatise" is taken as Zhedang Dorje's root text, compiled from these sources. "The Essence of the Teachings, set out as ground, path and fruition" reads bstan pa'i snying po as the root text's title (Theg chen bstan pa'i snying po); it can also be read "the essence, taught as ground, path and fruition".
Q: The vaidurya here functions as the wish-fulfilling jewel.

### U12
TEXT:
The purpose is to understand these through study and reflection. The ultimate purpose is that, by meditating on their meaning, the fruition becomes manifest. The connection: each later one depends on the one before it, so that they are linked in sequence as the means and what arises from the means.
NOTES:
Q: dgos pa'i dgos pa ("the purpose of the purpose") rendered with the usual English "ultimate purpose".
