# CT_tantra — bare run (no skill), Claude Opus at xhigh effort

### U01
TEXT:
Through union with her the yogin
swiftly gains siddhi, beyond doubt;
or else, apart from a consort,
let him meditate in deep equipoise.
NOTES:
Q: "union with her" takes the opening phrase as union with the consort (the wisdom partner) named in the preceding span, which the contrast in line 3 seems to require; it could also be read more generally as "by that practice".
Q: "consort" renders the Tibetan word for wisdom (prajna), used here in its tantric sense of the female partner.

### U03
TEXT:
Then the Blessed Lady asked:
What is the deities' purity?
O Guide, I long to hear of it.
The mandalas you taught before,
Lord, teach me now their purity.
Then the Blessed One said:
NOTES:
Q: the questioner is feminine (the Blessed Lady), presumably the consort Dveshavajri; the reply is spoken by the Blessed One.
Q: "purity" renders the hermeneutical term for the symbolic correspondence of each element (Sanskrit vishuddhi), not ritual cleansing.

### U04

### U05
TEXT:
Here, the square is the four immeasurables. The four doors are the four truths. The four porticoes are the four dhyanas. The eight pillars are the noble eightfold path. The single tier is one-pointedness of mind. The lotus is the womb. The various colors are the various emanations. The nine deities are the nine branches of scripture. The red of the cardinal quarters is great passion. The white, yellow, green and black in the intermediate quarters are the brahmin, vaishya, kshatriya and shudra castes. The moon and sun are semen and sweat. The sword at the center is the emblem of the black Acala. The curved knife is Dveshavajri. The eastern and other cardinal quarters are the white Acala and the rest. The southeastern and other intermediate quarters belong to Mohavajri and the rest. This is the purity of the mandala.
NOTES:
Q: "single tier" is a guess for an architectural feature of the palace (a single ledge, step or enclosure); the exact element is uncertain.
Q: "the red of the cardinal quarters" reads the phrase as the color of the four main directions, set against the four colors of the corners; it could also mean a single red quarter.
Q: "semen and sweat": one expects semen and the female red element (cf. U06, "the white and the red"); the word read here as "sweat" may stand for that, or the text may be corrupt. The same pair recurs in U09.
Q: the Tibetan spelling of "shudra" is broken by a stray syllable mark; read as the standard word.

### U06
TEXT:
The purity of the meditation will now be stated. First, the offering is the act of the exalted accumulation of merit. Emptiness is the accumulation of wisdom and the exalted death; the stainless body is the body of the gandharva, the being between death and birth. The celestial palace is the final goal, the ground of buddhahood. The lotus is the womb. The moon and sun are the white and the red.
NOTES:
Q: the clause on emptiness is syntactically loose; it may instead mean "emptiness is the accumulation of wisdom, the exalted death, the stainless body, that is, the body of the gandharva".
Q: "final goal" renders a phrase meaning "the ultimate end" or "culmination"; it may qualify the palace (its completion) rather than stand in apposition to the buddha ground.

### U07
TEXT:
The syllable HUM is the mind of the gandharva between the mother and the father. Akshobhya is the father; Mamaki is the mother. Seeing the two in mutual passion, it feels aversion toward the father and passion toward the mother, and through delusion it enters as an ordinary being does. The son who has entered the lotus kills the father and gains his place. Taking the mother, through the exalted bliss of the imprints of endless births, he in turn begets a son. The sons, in turn, are the white Acala and the rest; the daughters are Mohavajri and the rest. The sons too kill the father and go in to another; it is thinking "This is the enemy" that they kill him. The daughters too, through the exalted bliss of the imprints of endless births, feel desire.
NOTES:
Q: the Tibetan reads "the daughters, in turn, are the white Acala and the rest, and also Mohavajri and the rest"; since the Acalas are male, "sons" seems to have dropped out, and the sentence is rendered as sons = Acalas, daughters = Mohavajri and the rest.
Q: "he in turn begets a son" could also mean "he too is born as a son"; the reading chosen fits the cycle continued in the next sentences.
Q: "go in to another" takes the verb in its sexual sense; it could simply mean "pass on to another".

### U08
TEXT:
The sword is wisdom and the noose is method; or else the noose is wisdom and the sword is method. Their becoming one taste is the threatening forefinger. The left eye gazing downward guards the seven underworlds; the right gazing upward guards the seven cosmic eggs of Brahma. The left knee planted on the ground guards the earth. Striking firmly with the right foot terrifies all the maras. The mara of the aggregates is Brahma; the mara of the afflictions is Ishvara; the mara of the lord of death is Vishnu; the mara of the son of the gods is Indra.
NOTES:
Q: "eye" is supplied; the Tibetan says only "the left gazing downward" and "the right gazing upward", which fits the Acala iconography of one eye looking down and one looking up.
Q: the Tibetan has two misspellings, read as "wisdom" (for the noose clause) and "knee".

### U09
TEXT:
The earth is the enjoyment of the young women of the whole face of the earth. The lotus seat means that he dwells long within young women. Birth from the womb is the seat of moon and sun. The man born of semen and sweat is existence; the woman is nonexistence. Black is consciousness; white is form; yellow is feeling; red is perception; green is Amoghasiddhi.
NOTES:
Q: "the earth" presumably glosses "guards the earth" in U08.
Q: in the list of aggregates one expects "green is formations"; the text names Amoghasiddhi, the buddha of that family, which may be a corruption or a deliberate shift.
