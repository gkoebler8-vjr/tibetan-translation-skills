### U01
TEXT:
To him who, for his own sake, abides as the dharmakaya, without transition or change — the element, completely pure by nature, in which the adventitious stains are utterly stilled, not compounded by causes and conditions, free from elaboration like space — within the equality of samsara and nirvana;
NOTES:
Q: The particle after "pure by nature" is read as "in which" (the naturally pure element in which the adventitious stains are stilled, the twofold purity); it may be a plain "and". "Like space" can also qualify "abides".

### U05
TEXT:
and who, displaying the twelve deeds, moistens with the rain of the sacred Dharma of peace and bliss the mindstreams of those to be trained whose potential is truly awakened, and brings them to liberation — to the Lion of the Shakyas, foremost of the sugatas, I bow in reverence with body, speech and mind.
NOTES:
Q: "Potential" renders rigs (spiritual family, disposition); the rain of Dharma can also be read as what awakens it, not only what moistens the mindstream.
Q: "Lion of the Shakyas" translates the name Shakya Senge; use "Shakyasimha" if the house keeps the Sanskrit name.

### U06
TEXT:
As the Uttaratantra says:
The Knower of Worlds, in great compassion,
beholding all the worlds, and yet
unmoving from the dharmakaya,
through emanations of every kind —
NOTES:
Q: 'jig rten mkhyen is read as the epithet "Knower of Worlds" (Sanskrit lokavid) and taken as the subject of the whole citation; it can also be read "in great compassion he knows the world".
Q: Short title kept as "Uttaratantra"; change it if the glossary fixes an English title.

### U07
TEXT:
his births, his birth among the gods,
descending from the Tushita realm,
entering the womb, and being born,
delighting in his retinue of queens,
NOTES:
Q: skye ba mngon par skye ba is literally "birth, manifest birth"; it is read with the Sanskrit (jataka, upapatti) as his births and his birth among the gods in Tushita. Some take it as a heading for the deeds that follow.
Q: The canonical text has one more pada after the womb-and-birth line, bzo yi gnas la mkhas pa dang, mastery of the arts and crafts. The Tibetan as given omits it, so one of the twelve deeds is missing. Restore it? Line ready: "his mastery of arts and crafts,".

### U10
TEXT:
Second: From the scriptures of the Supreme Sage, victorious over the four maras — the aggregates, the lord of death, the son of the gods and the afflictions — scriptures free from the extremes of eternalism and nihilism and immeasurable as the depths of the ocean; from the sutras and tantras, the treatises of Nagarjuna, Asanga and others, and the meaning of the profound pith instructions of the great Dharma Lord Drikungpa, worthy of veneration like the jewel on the crown of the naga king — from among all these this text has been condensed, free from the stains of incomprehension, misconception and doubt. Just as everything one needs and wishes for comes forth when one prays to the vaidurya, supreme among jewels, so this treatise accomplishes every aim, temporary and ultimate. I shall explain this Heart of the Teachings, set forth as ground, path and fruition.
NOTES:
Q: "Second" is the outline marker for the commitment to explain; keep it, or set a heading.
Q: chos rje 'bri gung pa chen po, Jigten Sumgön, is kept as "the great Dharma Lord Drikungpa", as the source names him.
Q: The naga-king simile may qualify all the sources listed, not only Drikungpa's instructions.
Q: "Vaidurya" is kept (beryl or lapis lazuli); use "beryl" if the house prefers English.
Q: "Heart of the Teachings" echoes the root text's title, Theg chen bstan pa'i snying po, and is capitalized as a title.
