U01: The liberation of the śrāvakas follows after sounds; that of the pratyekabuddhas arises from dependence; the liberation of the irreproachable buddhas is stainless, like the sky, having completely transcended attachment.
U02: The mind has not been bound from the beginning; these continuums of mind are naturally liberated. Just as they are liberated, so too does one truly know; having known, there is no decline whatsoever in that liberation.
U03: Furthermore, son of a noble family, all physical actions of the Tathāgata are preceded by primordial wisdom and followed by primordial wisdom. Possessing such physical actions, the Tathāgata tames sentient beings by his gaze; he tames sentient beings by speaking; he tames sentient beings by remaining silent; he tames sentient beings by eating; he tames sentient beings by his deportment; he tames sentient beings by his major marks; he tames sentient beings by his minor marks; he tames sentient beings by the invisibility of his crown; he tames sentient beings merely by being looked upon; he tames sentient beings by emitting light; he tames sentient beings by taking steps; and he tames sentient beings by entering and leaving cities. There is no deportment of the blessed Buddhas that does not serve to tame sentient beings. Therefore, it is said that all physical actions of the Tathāgata are preceded by primordial wisdom and followed by primordial wisdom. This is the twenty-seventh activity of the Tathāgata. Regarding this, it is said as follows:
U04: By demonstrating the conduct of the Guides, and by walking, entering, and likewise emerging, and by means of the excellent minor marks, the major marks, and the crown protuberance, the Buddhas engage in taming sentient beings.
U05: Whenever the Guide emits radiance, at that time many beings become blissful; having been touched by the light, they proceed thoroughly toward being tamed. This is the activity of the supreme among the two-footed.
U06: Furthermore, O son of good family, all the verbal activities of the Tathāgata are preceded by wisdom and followed by wisdom. Why is that? The Tathāgata teaches a Dharma that bears fruit; he gives definitive predictions; he speaks words that are thoroughly certain. That speech of the Tathāgata is also comprehensive, instructive, not haughty, not base, not evasive, not stuttering, not devious, not harsh, not rough, not confused, gentle, pleasant to hear, workable, not incoherent, not impulsive, not hurried, not overly quick, not excessively rapid, thoroughly analytical, thoroughly certain in expression, productive of great joy, extremely melodious, extremely clear, unimpeded, complete, sweet, connected, proclaimed, worthy of veneration, beautiful, dustless, free from dust, without dross, stainless, free from stains, without delusion, thoroughly perfected, without attachment, fully liberated, luminous, upright, not inferior, not muddled, not indistinct, productive of happiness, satisfying to the body, productive of joy in the mind, pacifying desire, subduing hatred, dispelling ignorance, conquering Māra, removing evil, subduing opponents, and instructive. It is the sound of a drum, productive of joy for the wise, the melody of the kalaviṅka, the sound of Śakra, the sound of Brahmā, the sound of the ocean’s waves, the sound of clouds, the melody of the earth, the sound of the crane, the cry of the peacock, the sound of the cuckoo, the sound of the śaṅśaṅte'u, the sound of the king of geese, the sound of the king of beasts, and the sound of the lute, the three-stringed lute, the drum, the flute, the conch, and the cymbals. It is to be fully understood, to be thoroughly known, clear, pleasant, worthy of being heard, profound, not false, pleasing to the ears, productive of the roots of virtue, without loss of words or letters, composed of words and letters that are thoroughly certain, meaningful, endowed with Dharma, timely, endowed with all proper occasions, and unmoving from the proper time.
U07: It is the teaching of the knowledge of superior and non-superior faculties. It is adorned by generosity. It is purified by ethical discipline. It is perfectly accomplished by patience. It is thoroughly refined by diligence. It is made serviceable by meditative concentration. It is definitively established by wisdom. It is thoroughly initiated by loving-kindness. It is untiring through compassion. It is joyful. It is luminous. It is definitively examined by equanimity. It is the setting forth of the three vehicles. It is the non-interruption of the lineage of the Three Jewels. It is the analysis of the three aggregates. It is purified by the three doors of liberation. It is thoroughly suffused with truth. It is thoroughly suffused with primordial wisdom. It is not disparaged by the wise. It is praised by the noble ones. It follows the measure of space. It possesses the excellence of all aspects.
U08: Son of noble family, the speech of the Tathāgata is such as this; therefore, it is said that all verbal activities of the Tathāgata are preceded by primordial wisdom and followed by primordial wisdom. This is the twenty-eighth activity of the Tathāgata. Regarding that, it is said as follows:
U09: The incomparable speech is clear, stainless, and exalted by all virtues; it fills a thousand worlds extensively, and in a single instant, all languages resound. Some sentient beings hear the Śrāvaka vehicle, and likewise, some hear the Pratyekabuddha vehicle; having heard this, the Buddhas, the great beings, direct their minds toward this very awakening.
U10: The syllables are not connected, nor do they become mutually contradictory. Although they teach the Dharma in this way, the mind does not become conceptual. Just as an echo resounds without movement and without an agent, so too do the best of men utter sounds and satisfy sentient beings.
U11: Furthermore, O son of noble family, all of the Tathāgata’s mental activities are preceded by wisdom and followed by wisdom. Why is that? The Tathāgata is not to be expressed by mind, not by intellect, and not by consciousness; rather, the Tathāgata is distinguished by wisdom and has become the master of wisdom. That wisdom of the Tathāgata follows after the minds of all sentient beings. It enters into the intellects of all sentient beings. It is the awareness that knows all sentient beings. It has definitively contemplated all phenomena. It does not rely on others for any meditative absorption. It has completely transcended objects of focus. It is free from arising due to conditions. It has eliminated the three realms of existence. It has completely transcended all levels of pride. It is liberated from all the activities of Māra. It is free from all deceit and guile. It has abandoned the grasping at "I" and the grasping at "mine." It is free from the darkness of ignorance and confusion, and from the cataracts of blurred vision. It has completely cultivated the limbs of the path. It is non-conceptual like the sky. It is inseparable from the dharmadhātu. Such is the Tathāgata’s mental activity that is preceded by wisdom.
