U01: The Victor, the Lord of bipeds, cannot be measured by mind or intellect. Through completely pure primordial wisdom, the activities of all Guides are purified. That Sugata-gnosis enters into the intentions of all sentient beings with equanimity, and it is the nature of the *dharmadhātu*.
U02: Possessing fully the meditative concentrations and likewise all the samādhis, The discursive thoughts of the mind and mental faculty do not circulate. Liberated from the domain of Māra and having completely transcended the activities of Māra, It is equal to the nature of space, stainless, and without change.
U03: Furthermore, son of noble family, the wisdom of the Tathāgata regarding the past enters without attachment and without obstruction. How does it enter? He fully knows, by way of enumeration, all the buddhafields of the past, however many have arisen and perished. He fully knows all those that have become grass, trees, shrubs, medicinal herbs, and forests in those buddhafields. He fully knows all the multitudes of sentient beings, however many have been designated as sentient beings in those buddhafields. He fully knows all their various modes of conduct, however many there are. He fully knows all the buddhas, however many have appeared in those buddhafields. He fully knows all the Dharma that has been taught and spoken by each and every buddha, however much there is. He fully knows all the sentient beings, however many have been trained in the Śrāvaka vehicle. He fully knows all the sentient beings, however many have been trained in the Pratyekabuddha vehicle. He fully knows all the sentient beings, however many have been trained in the Great Vehicle. He fully knows all the distinctions of the buddhafields. He also fully knows the distinctions of the communities of monks. He also fully knows the distinctions of the lifespans. He also fully knows the distinctions of the duration of the Dharma. He also fully knows the exhalation and inhalation of breath. He also fully knows the enjoyment of the finest food.
U04: The Tathāgata fully knows the past states of all sentient beings, as well as their deaths and their rebirths. He also fully knows their various faculties, various behaviors, and various inclinations. He also fully knows their mental continuums. The Tathāgata fully knows, by way of enumeration through direct perception and inferential knowledge, exactly which mental state arises following which mental state; and those past mental continuums of the Tathāgata that have been relinquished are also perceived. Because the Tathāgata possesses such wisdom, he teaches the Dharma to sentient beings precisely according to their inclinations. This is the thirtieth activity of the Tathāgata. Regarding this, it is said as follows:
U05: Regarding the fields, sentient beings, and the teachers of the Dharma, the Buddha’s wisdom is unattached and also immeasurable; and the number of those sentient beings whom the Guides have liberated, and the number of those who have become the monastic assembly of monks in the fields,
U06: Whatever the intentions and faculties of sentient beings may be, and whatever the gradations of their forms and sounds, and their minds, their names, and all their past activities—the perfect Buddha realizes them through correct vision.
U07: Furthermore, son of noble family, the Tathāgata’s vision of wisdom enters into the future time without attachment and without obstruction. How does it enter? The Tathāgata thoroughly knows all those beings, phenomena, or fields that will arise, cease, come into existence, or be destroyed in the future. He knows however many will be consumed by the fires of the eon, however many will be destroyed by water, however many Buddha-fields will persist, however many will become earth elements in those Buddha-fields, however many will become minute particles of dust, however many will become grasses, trees, branches, medicinal herbs, and forests, and however many will become the forms of stars.
U08: The Tathāgata thoroughly knows however many Buddhas will arise in each of the infinite fields, however many śrāvakas will arise, however many pratyekabuddhas will arise, however many bodhisattvas will arise, and however many enjoyments of supreme food, inhalations, exhalations, walking, sitting, and modes of conduct there will be. He thoroughly knows all the ways in which each of the infinite Tathāgatas will cause sentient beings to be liberated through the śrāvaka vehicle, the pratyekabuddha vehicle, and the Great Vehicle. The Tathāgata thoroughly knows however many places of birth for sentient beings there are in each of the infinite fields, and however many minds and mental factors will arise; yet, the Tathāgata’s own future stream of mind does not become subject to the eons, and by viewing the future, he teaches the Dharma to sentient beings. This is the thirty-first activity of the Tathāgata. Regarding this, it is said:
U09: With stainless eyes, in the future time, [the Buddha] knows all that will slightly arise and perish, and all the Victors in all the Buddha-fields, without exception.
U10: When directing attention toward the future, their minds are never distracted; likewise, they teach the Dharma to sentient beings, and this is the activity of the supreme among the two-footed.
U11: Furthermore, O son of good family, the Tathāgata’s vision of wisdom enters into the present time without attachment and without obstruction. How does it enter? In this regard, the Tathāgata thoroughly knows all present buddha-fields in the ten directions by way of three modes of counting. He thoroughly knows all present buddhas. He thoroughly knows all present bodhisattvas. He thoroughly knows all present śrāvakas. He thoroughly knows all present pratyekabuddhas.
