U01: Furthermore, O son of good family, the Tathāgata’s meditative stabilization (*samādhi*) does not degenerate. That which is the Tathāgata’s meditative stabilization is the sameness of all phenomena, the non-inequality of all phenomena, and the suchness of all phenomena. If one asks, "Why does the Tathāgata’s meditative stabilization not degenerate?" it is because that which is equal to suchness (*tathatā*) is equal to meditative stabilization. That which is equal to meditative stabilization is equal to the Tathāgata; therefore, it is called "established in equilibrium" (*samāhita*).
U02: Whatever the limit of desire is equal to, the limit of freedom from desire is also equal to that. Whatever the limit of hatred is equal to, the limit of freedom from hatred is also equal to that. Whatever the limit of delusion is equal to, the limit of freedom from delusion is also equal to that. Whatever the limit of the conditioned is equal to, the limit of the unconditioned is also equal to that. Whatever the limit of cyclic existence is equal to, the limit of nirvana is also equal to that; because one has entered into the equanimity of those equalities, therefore it is called "equipoise" (*samāhita*). Therefore, it is said that the meditative concentration (*samādhi*) of the Tathāgata does not decline. Why is that? Because that equality does not decline, and it does not completely decline.
U03: That samādhi of the Tathāgata is not associated with the eye, nor is it associated with the ear, the nose, the tongue, the body, or the mind; yet, the Tathāgata is not lacking in sense faculties. The samādhi of the Tathāgata does not abide in the earth element, it does not abide in the water element, it does not abide in the fire element, and it does not abide in the wind element. It does not abide in the desire realm, it does not abide in the form realm, and it does not abide in the formless realm. It does not abide in this world, and it does not abide in the world beyond. Because it does not abide, it does not degenerate; therefore, it is said that the samādhi of the Tathāgata does not degenerate. The Tathāgata teaches the Dharma so that all sentient beings may also attain samādhi; this is the twenty-fourth activity of the Tathāgata. Regarding that, it is said as follows:
U04: The Buddha is always in meditative equipoise; his concentration does not diminish. He is endowed with equality regarding all phenomena; the Buddha is poised in the state of equality.
U05: It is not in earth, water, or fire; the Victorious One abides in the wind, the desire realm, the form realm, and the formless realm, but not in meditative concentration; because of that, this meditative absorption is not impaired.
U06: Furthermore, O son of good family, the Tathāgata’s wisdom is never diminished. What is this wisdom? It is the wisdom that thoroughly understands all phenomena, a knowledge that does not rely upon others. It is the knowledge of teaching the Dharma correctly to other sentient beings and individuals. It is the knowledge of being skilled in the analytical knowledges (*pratisaṃvid*), thereby knowing how to distinguish words. It is the knowledge of teaching while remaining within a single word for a hundred thousand aeons. It is the knowledge that cuts through doubts exactly as they are asked. It is the knowledge of being unattached in all respects. It is the knowledge of teaching the arrangement of the three vehicles. It is the knowledge that fully understands the eighty-four thousand patterns of the mental conduct of sentient beings. It is the knowledge of teaching the Dharma, each according to its own kind, regarding the eighty-four thousand aggregates of Dharma. This is the teaching of the Tathāgata’s wisdom as boundless, bottomless, inexhaustible, and immeasurable.
U07: Just as the Tathāgata is without decline and without total deterioration, so too does he teach the Dharma in order that the wisdom of all sentient beings may become inexhaustible; this is the twenty-fifth enlightened activity of the Tathāgata. Regarding that, it is said as follows:
U08: Through wisdom, the modes of reality are directly perceived; having reached the perfection of the power of the specific correct understandings, one intentionally and spontaneously teaches the Dharma to sentient beings, and within a single syllable, infinite words are contained.
U09: Having perfectly understood the boundless conduct of beings, and likewise teaching eighty-four thousand dharmas, the wisdom of the Sugatas never diminishes; this activity of the ten powers is superior to the actions of beings.
U10: Furthermore, O son of good family, the liberation of the Tathāgata is never lost. What is the liberation of the Tathāgata? The liberation of the Śrāvakas is to follow the word; the liberation of the Pratyekabuddhas is to understand conditions; the liberation of the Buddhas is called "liberation" because it is free from all attachment and grasping. It is not bound by the past, it does not shift into the future, and it does not abide in the present. It is liberation from the dualistic grasping of eye and form. It is liberation from the dualistic grasping of ear and sound, nose and smell, tongue and taste, body and touch, and mind and phenomena; it is liberation from the state of being fixated on grasping. One knows the mind to be luminous by nature in that way; therefore, because of the wisdom that possesses a single moment of mind, it is said that one has fully awakened to the unsurpassed, perfect, and complete enlightenment.
