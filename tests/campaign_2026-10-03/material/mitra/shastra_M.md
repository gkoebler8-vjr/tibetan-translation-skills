# MITRA (dm.py translate, style: literal, keep every clause and connective) — shastra
U01 At that time, regarding the statement that "the Blessed One addressed the venerable Śāradvatīputra," if many have all gathered there, why would he address only one? This also contradicts what was stated above, that [each person] thinks, "The Tathāgata is sitting before me and teaching the Dharma." If all those gathered there think, "The Tathāgata is teaching the Dharma, looking toward me alone," then if the Blessed One were to specifically address one person from among them all, how would that not contradict the minds of the assembly? 
U02 Here it is said: Although the Tathāgata explains by initiating from all and actualizing all, in the very moment he speaks to Śāriputra, he is also speaking to all; and in that very moment of speaking, he performs various distinct explanations for all. However, because the compilers are unable to perfectly compile that sequence, they set aside the splendor of the wisdom of the explanation and, having adopted a single sequence suitable for all beings, they have compiled it perfectly. 
U03 Alternatively, because the Tathāgata taught [this] having initiated it from Śāriputra himself, and because this very method is a cause for the holy Dharma to remain for a long time, the Tathāgata also granted permission. Therefore, those who perform the compilation also compiled it by relying upon this very [method]. 
U04 Even if that is the case, why, when teaching the supreme explanation of the Bodhisattva vehicle, were the Bodhisattvas set aside, and the discourse addressed specifically to Śāriputra, and the occasion opened for him? 
U05 It should be understood in this way: the explanation of the perfection of wisdom is a general explanation; it is not explained solely for the sake of the bodhisattvas. Why is that? Because the perfection of wisdom resides in the three aspects: the knowledge of all [phenomena], the knowledge of the path, and the knowledge of all aspects. In this regard, if the Blessed One were to give a teaching and initiate the discourse by addressing any bodhisattva, it is possible that other members of the assembly might think, "This profound [teaching] is not within our range of experience," and thus not exert themselves. However, if the discourse is initiated by addressing Śāriputra, others might think, "This explanation is common to all people and is not extremely profound." Therefore, the discourse was initiated by addressing Śāriputra himself. Just as it appears later, it is also stated that the monks and other members of the assembly, having realized the Dharma, attained it individually. 
U06 Even if that is so, since the Elder Śāriputra has done what was to be done, it is not appropriate to make a distinction starting from this great exposition of the meaning; if one were to ask why, [it is because...] 
U07 Regarding the statement that the Elder Śāriputra had accomplished his task, the Bhagavan, in the *Saddharmapuṇḍarīka-sūtra*, prophesied that the Elder Śāriputra would become a buddha, saying: "Śāriputra, in a future time, after an immeasurable, inconceivable, and limitless number of eons, having upheld the sublime Dharma of many hundreds of thousands of millions of billions of tathāgatas and having performed various kinds of offerings, and having brought these very bodhisattva practices to full completion, you will appear in the world as a tathāgata, arhat, perfectly enlightened buddha named Padmaprabha, in a world system called Virajā." 
U08 How is it that a monk whose outflows have ceased, whose karma and mental afflictions have been severed, links to a new existence and engages in the conduct of a bodhisattva for inconceivable and immeasurable eons? This, too, should not be understood [as such]. If it were the case that even arhats link to a new existence, then all arhats would individually take on new existences, and it would follow that liberation is not attained—is this what you are asking? 
U09 Furthermore, this is not certain; for if one were to argue that "if Arhats possessed karma and *kleśas*, then by the power of that karma and those *kleśas* they would also be reborn in existence, and thus liberation would not be achieved," that would be logical. However, one does not assert that Arhats possess karma and *kleśas*, nor that they are reborn in existence caused by them. 
U10 The meaning shall be explained extensively according to the sequence appearing in other sūtras. In this regard, the Tathāgata has correctly perceived that some sentient beings are of a determinate lineage, and some are of an indeterminate lineage.
