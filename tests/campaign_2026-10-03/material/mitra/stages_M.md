# MITRA (dm.py translate, style: literal, keep every clause and connective) — stages
U01 The Blessed One taught that there is no fixed order as to which of the three precious trainings must come first, and that they are taught in various ways. 
U02 If one thinks, "If it is like that, what is to be done with these various disagreements that have become the basis for dispute?" then, just as was stated above, is it not the case that one should enter through those eighty-four thousand gateways of the Dharma that have been taught and reach their ultimate depth? 
U03 Is it not the case that the gateway of intention is the gateway of entry? By entering through that which is the gateway of intention, [one enters]. 
U04 The complete abandoning of afflictions and conceptual thoughts is the higher training in discipline. 
U05 Entering through that door, the arrival inside that door into the home of liberation is the higher training in meditative concentration. 
U06 Is not the higher training of wisdom the single flavor, in the aspect of complete liberation, of all phenomena of samsara and nirvana that lie at the depth of that door? 
U07 If this is the case, then for those who have entered the gate of the precious teachings of the Tathāgata and have gone for refuge in the Three Jewels, even though the various particulars of their practices are not the same but are different, all of them are similarly Dharma; yet, by saying, "Only this of mine is Dharma, that of his is not Dharma; the Nyingma Secret Mantra is not Dharma; that of Vajrapāṇi is not Dharma; the mental non-engagement of *amanasikāra* is not Dharma," and so forth, they disparage the words of the Tathāgata. 
U08 Having made the three—attachment, aversion, and delusion—the cause, and having ripened into the result of samsara and the lower realms, one is oneself an object of compassion; therefore, 
U09 By means of the eye of wisdom consisting of the three—listening, contemplating, and meditating—one sees many of the Sugata’s scriptures; and upon hearing them, one does not disparage anything, and having found that eye, and having become learned and attained understanding, one becomes a great *kalyāṇamitra* (spiritual friend) for all those who uphold them. 
U10 In this way also, the Bhagavan stated in the *Avadānaśataka*: "Those who abide in calm abiding (*śamatha*) will be completely liberated by special insight (*vipaśyanā*); those who abide in special insight will be completely liberated by calm abiding; and those who abide in them equally will be completely liberated by the elements (*dhātu*)." 
U11 And because the precious lama, the protector of the three worlds, has also stated: "The root of virtue of skillful means is enhanced by wisdom; the root of virtue of wisdom is enhanced by skillful means." 
U12 Because the entry-points for the minds of individuals are diverse, the practice of diverse methods of Dharma serves as an antidote to afflictions and conceptual thoughts; and that all phenomena become of one taste within the great ocean of liberation and complete liberation is the subject matter of the three baskets—the essential points of all the scriptures of the Buddha, the Bhagavan—the stages of practice, that which generates joy for the wise, that which causes the foolish to become bewildered, and the domain of the realizations of the profound and the vast; one should fully master this alone.
