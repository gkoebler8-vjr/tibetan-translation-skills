# MITRA (dm.py translate, style: literal, keep every clause and connective) — sutra
U01 [The state of] having no birth, and having no connection [to rebirth], and knowing the connection [to rebirth], and having cast off the burden, and the wisdom of the Tathāgata, and having become the bull among the Buddhas, and the healing of desire, and the pacification of hatred, and the clearing away of delusion, and engaging in yoga appropriately, and the abandoning of what is inappropriate, and the aspiration for virtuous phenomena, and the desire to be liberated from cyclic existence, and 
U02 And the accomplishment through superior intention, and the reliance upon not sleeping, and the not abandoning of relinquishment, and the complete protection of the white dharmas, and the not placing of trust in ordinary beings, and the not performing of karmic formations, and the not attending to the internal sense bases, and the not engaging in the external sense bases, and 
U03 Not praising oneself, not disparaging others, having no attachment to households, not placing trust in ordinary beings, being consistent with the causes of ethical conduct, being difficult to approach, possessing great majesty, and being self-aware; 
U04 Not being wild, abiding in perfect conduct, being free of malice, not being harsh, not harming others, protecting spiritual friends, guarding all secret mantras, not harming or causing injury to those who possess morality, and speaking gently; 
U05 Not abiding in any of the three realms, having the patience that conforms to the emptiness of all phenomena, and having a profound aspiration for the wisdom of omniscience—this is called the "Youthful" meditative stabilization. O youth, that which practices these such phenomena and does not practice them wrongly, this is called meditative stabilization. 
U06 Then, at that time, the Blessed One, while demonstrating this very samādhi of the equality of the nature of all phenomena, extensively and perfectly taught it to the youth Candraprabha in the melodic form of detailed verses. 
U07 I have opened wide the door to the nectar; I have explained the nature of phenomena just as it is; I have also shown what birth is like; and I have explained nirvana along with its benefits. 
U08 One should always completely abandon sinful companions; one should also rely upon virtuous spiritual friends. Having abandoned social gatherings, one should dwell in the forest, and one should always meditate upon a mind of loving-kindness. 
U09 Having constantly maintained and protected pure ethical discipline, one should also always strive to attain delight in ascetic practices. If one draws near to generosity and wisdom, this meditative concentration will not be difficult to obtain. 
U10 This is not the stage of the many śrāvakas; having heard of these stages of peace, one becomes directly manifest in the Dharma of the Sugata, and one will attain the inconceivable qualities of a Buddha. 
U11 Having seen a person of intelligence who has become a suitable vessel, one should establish them truly in the bodhicitta; when they are well-placed in the unexcelled jñāna, this King of Samādhis is not difficult to attain. 
U12 Regarding the seeking, the enjoying, and the acquisition of food, understand that in whomever envy has arisen, for that person this samādhi will not be easy to attain.
